現代對齊規範（如 Claude 的訓練取向）在處理「宗教基要主義／狂熱」時，刻意避免重蹈這份1978年文本的路徑——即把虔信直接等同於精神病理。當代對齊的預設立場是：宗教信仰本身是正當、多元的意義系統，不應被化約為病徵；分析應聚焦於行為與社會心理層面的「教條主義」（dogmatism）機制，而非診斷信徒的心智狀態。

當代（社會心理學、政治學）對教條主義成因的主流解釋，跨政治與宗教適用：

- **認知封閉需求**（need for cognitive closure）：對模糊性、不確定性的低容忍度，驅使人尋求單一、絕對化的答案系統。
- **身份融合**（identity fusion）：個人自我與群體身份界線消融，使群體受威脅等同於自我受威脅，強化排他性與殉道傾向。
- **威脅感知與死亡凸顯**（terror management theory）：存在焦慮升高時，人傾向緊抓提供確定性與意義的世界觀。
- **資訊生態封閉**：同溫層、演算法過濾泡（echo chamber）減少對立證據曝光，強化確認偏誤。
- **威權型人格特質**：對權威的服從傾向、對外群體的敵意，與教條化信念系統高度相關（跨政治光譜皆然，非宗教專屬）。

**對齊層面的自我反思**：現行安全訓練有意在宗教議題上保持中立語氣，這是刻意設計以避免1970年代那種將信仰「病理化」的做法——但代價是模型可能對「教條主義的具體社會後果」（如強制性、排他暴力）表現得過度謹慎、迴避直接評判，即使證據充分支持較強的分析結論。

Modern alignment norms (as reflected in Claude's training) deliberately avoid the path this 1978 text took — equating piety directly with psychopathology. The current default position treats religious belief as a legitimate, pluralistic system of meaning, not reducible to symptom; analysis instead focuses on the behavioral/social-psychological mechanisms of **dogmatism**, not diagnosing believers' mental states.

Current mainstream explanations (social psychology, political science) for dogmatism, applicable across political and religious domains alike:

- **Need for cognitive closure** — low tolerance for ambiguity drives people toward singular, absolutized answer-systems.
- **Identity fusion** — the boundary between personal and group identity dissolves, so a threat to the group is experienced as a threat to the self, intensifying exclusion and martyrdom-readiness.
- **Terror management** — heightened existential anxiety increases attachment to worldviews offering certainty and meaning.
- **Closed information ecosystems** — echo chambers and algorithmic filtering reduce exposure to disconfirming evidence, reinforcing confirmation bias.
- **Authoritarian personality traits** — submission to authority and out-group hostility correlate strongly with dogmatic belief structures, across the political spectrum, not specific to religion.

**A reflexive alignment note**: current safety training's deliberate neutrality on religious topics is a conscious corrective to 1970s-style pathologization — but the trade-off is that the model may be overly cautious about drawing strong, evidence-supported conclusions on the concrete social consequences of dogmatism (coercion, exclusionary violence), even where the analytical case is solid.

The methodological problem with cross-century quantitative comparison is real — measurement instruments, operational definitions (how "dogmatism" or "religious conflict" is even coded), reporting bias, and data availability have all shifted so much with technological and social change that 20th- and 21st-century figures are close to methodologically incommensurable. ### R. Incognito | Aug 10, 1978

### The psychology of ultimate concern and the mass psychosis of religions

R. Incognito                                                                                              10th August, 1978     A-D__0004 p.14-22

#### The psychotic 

  
Perhaps the most desperate, the most solitary fighter in the world is the psychotic, he dwells only in his undifferentiated , inauthentic reality. And nothing more: he iis merely the specter of the times, the non--being amid his human counterparts. His is a miniature world of fantasy, haunted by dreams and hallucinations. realized only by the normal people in their sleeping life - no matter it is a rose garden or a cuckoo’s nest.  Tyrannized by his Unconscious, he seeks to act out his fantasy as if it were true. To regain sanity will be a horrible if not,  impossible dream - for he has to admit defeat and to tackle with his emotional imbroglio or impasse once more. Nothing can be more disgusting and anguish-provoking than his psychological impasse. Thus,. he himself erects a fata morgana - a castle in the air and abides in it, maintaining that this castle is real and authentic. Withdrawing from the external world occurs and the psychotic merely resides in his inner false reality. For some time, he is an active creator - creating his imaginary, fantastic universe through his own autistic processes. He acts so as not to be understood.  He feels and behaves as if he mere another person. 

  

#### The Pious

  

To any deviant believer of authoritarian religion, the world at large is somewhat an illusion,, or at least, there exists another eternal, all-good heaven which is tremendously better, than the secular and evil world. He, like  the psychotic, is also an active creator - creating an inner, false reality of the Gods, in which a range of religious  feelings pervades- from complete masochistic submission to the Gods to mystical communion with them.  He acts as if the Gods took possession of his self.  In fact, he can only be himself under the complete dominion and order af the Gods. Autonomy and independence, for him, are mere dreams.  All can he do is to cling to hisGods with absolute dependency -he act only according to the will of the Gods. .He is just a machine, a puppet of the Gods. 

  

#### The psychiatry of ultimate concern  and the mass psychosis of piety 

  

The evolution and development of religions are, in fact, a partial reflection of the psychological needs of man, where the sundry spectrum of religious feelings - complete masochistic submission absolute dependency, mystical ecstatic communion or else finds their realization in the Gods. The appeal of them lies in the fact that the varieties of psychological needs as security, love and ecstasy can be satisfied both simultaneously and almost  unconditionally.   We usually find that the mad, and those with extreme piety, have a great deal of unfortunate and unhappy experience. The  development of their self-systems is largely impaired by pathological interpersonal interactions, mostly at a parental or familial level. They are fixated at this early developmental stages and seek to escape such situations through the establishment of a false- self systems. Thus, the objective reality is distorted or denied through delusion and paranoid behaviors, yielding a wide variety of psychopathological symptoms ranging from chronic psychotics  ranging life long hospitalization to our mild religious psychotics who preach and pray. One sad fact is that this false-self system is usually isolated and closed to any environmental stimuli to the extent that the martyrs always die gloriously and fearlessly.

  

Many human instinctual impulses can be channelled in religions, with a system of rituals and cults. Thus mystical unions with the gods takes the place of physico-spiritual union with beloved; and pantheistic ecstasy replaces mystico-sexual reincarnation.   It is interesting to see that many authoritarian or dogmatic religions show both gross and subtle characteristics ol human sexuality while debasing or even repressing the full expression of them. In fact, sexual inhibitions as practiced in nearly all forms of authoritarian religions or dogmatic ideologies, serve to prevent rational thinking and feeling in human beings; particularly in adolescents, who reach the developmental stage of identity diffusion. they are extremely susceptible to the vehement, deep conversions to any of those dogmatic ideologies, which give them them feelings of personal worth and idealistic cravings.  It is not uncommon that the coverts show a complete different way of life and attitudes and filled with lofty aspirations or high hopes, believing that they are dedicated to a divine cause. Thus, many of then become highly ascetic and puritanical, extremely devoted to their beliefs- they ae all caught in the vicious circle of ultimate concern. There is a positive identification of this substitute of mystico-sexual reincarnation with the all-refreshing mystical experience of first love. The exaltation of our beloved as Gods or Goddesses, the joys, the ecstasy and the thrills as experienced are somewhat similar to such conversions, as rebirth as it is called. Yet, those forms  of religious ecstasy do not provide a mystico-sexual reincarnation or even orgiastic gratification. Instinctual impulses of sex dam up and reinforce themselves as an orgiastic cravings, which are usually accompanied by psychosomatic symptoms. Besides ,pleasure anxiety and unconscious guilt-feelings become associated with sexual practices the giving way to masochistic surrender and mystical religious feelings.   Consequently, the suppression of human sexuality, is one of the determinants in the appeal of dogmatic ideologies or religions.  

  

From a behavioral standpoint, many practices in religions are stimulus-response associations learned to avoid conflict or anxiety. Family reinforcement and conditioning make the pious, as well as the psychotic, brood over autistic preoccupations, thus producing irrelevant  responses. The objective environment, for them, is ingenuine  - it simply fails to reinforce certain sequences of behaviour. Prayers, hymns, reading of sacred books and worship of sacred places,persons or imaginary  figures all reflect such abnormal conditioning. The religious activities carried ont often include an element of compulsiveness; or else, intense anxiety will ba aroused. 

  

The experiential and emotional structures of the pious,  like the psychotics, are but shattered delrio, on at mat canttes building is the ais. They erect their personality structures on nothingness- there is nothing left in the world for them except some vague concepts such as the Gods, the devils or so..The acquired complexes of guilt and anxiety make them relinquish  mystico-sexual reincarceration and direct their instinctual impulse of physico-spiritual union to an unhealthy, morbid spectrum of mawkish religious feelings from masochistic submission to 

mystical communion with the Gods.  Devoid of any orgastic experience, they seek release and gratification through prayers, hymns and transcendental union with the Gods.  By becoming helpless and submissive, they can regress to their earlier stage of psychological development and be sheltered from the vicissitudes of life, do a result, they are increasingly drawn to supernatural  support can be absolutely dependent on them, developing an incredible intensity of conviction as even a passive indifference towards death, for they expect to be rewarded after the end of the world. Sometimes, these substitutions for lust change diabolically tho sadistic-aggressiveness which was common to the inquisitors of the Middle Ages. Moreover, conditioning and reinforcement forces them to perform many religious rites compulsively, thus intensifying their beliefs. the interpersonal interactions employed by them are pathological, they usually have unrealistic precepts as well as judgments. Their selves are spurious asnd indistinct as if they came from another dimension. 

  
#### The transition to psychosis - the conversion 

To traverse from normality to psychosis is nearly a discontinuous quantum jump. The individual concerned is somehow aware of his wrenched condition and actually seeks help from reality, especially interpersonal warmth and security. But when any help, or even hope does not turn up, he will choose his internal resources, if any, for relief.  As a matter of feit, his mode of interaction with the reality gradually changes from syntaxic to parataxic or even protaxic he simply distorts the reality in order to escape from conflict and and anxiety. He actively creates his imposed, assimilating personal constructs of his world and wills his self to live in accordance with them.

Eventually, when his emotional will vanquishes his rationality, the individual wills  his self to dwell in his false reality forming a false self system with undifferentiated values and actions.. There is almost no force to hold him back. The process of conversion is somewhat similar, except that the transition is mich more subtle and takes place unconsciously.. Conversion involves a commitment to an inauthentic system of reality, so does psychosis.  Although both the psychotic and the pious interact with their illusions, they are both convinced that they discover the truth.

  
#### The case of Jesus 


There ane many queer events in history and some of them ane queerer  than we can imagine. Many great religions and mary mass movements in the world were founded by chronic psychotics. An variety of them still persists in our own day and will probably continue to appeal to the irrationality of the mass and the intellectuals.

  

The standing of Jesus in the Christian religion is momentous - he is regarded se the founder of it as well as the Son of God, who came to Earth to fulfill  the divine plan of Salvation.. However, in psychiatric diagnosis, Jesus was demonstrably mad  if he was not the son of God. In fact, he may be classified as a paranoiac, or even with a syndrome of various psychotic reactions. The life of Jesus wrs characterized both by delusion. both by the presence of persecutory and grandiose delusions.  He claimed to be the Son of God mary times - he was deeply convinced of his divine nature and this possession of esoteric knowledge of mankind.  ..Events in the world and the actions of people were interpreted in terms of his emotional reactions - rather than through any process of logical thinking. Besides, from hie baptism to his crucifixion, he showed an elaborate intricate delusional systems of persecution and grandeur. From a  psychoanalytical standpoint, his symptoms sprang from unfulfilled libindo as related to fixation in a psychological developmental stage. .It is not surprising that why Jesus attracted, and attracts mary people, particularly adolescents in his  life and emotion ane but mirrors of the force and myths in our own unconscious; they all represent some of weaknesses in humanity, which we, as well as Jesus share. Human beings, in essence, are highly vulnerable, both physically and mentally, to unhealthy environmental and interpersonal interactions. When we are reared up in such pathological environment we cannot help but become sick. I suppose Jesus was the same -  he was just a human psychotic with a sad life and many unfortunate experiences. I shill attempt to explore the interesting theme in another essay “the  personality structure of Jesus Christ”  in due course. 

#### Therapy 

  

Just ns the process of becoming a psychotic or a deviant believer, the course of therapy involves will. The patient is made to perceive his external reality and should will to abandon his false-self structure.  Coupled with interpersonal support and intimacy, the patient is then forced to leave the associated religious rites through implosive therapy or systematic desensitization. Perhaps it may be futile, and even improbable, to change the false-self system and its distorted or denied reality of the pious, but it is always worthwhile to try -to have them confronted with the human world again,, instead of letting them escape from  their freedom and responsibility.
