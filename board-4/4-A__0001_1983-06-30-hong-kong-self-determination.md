###  香港五百萬人自決前途

**關永祥
洪逸生**

在香港前途的問題上, 假若可以以一句話來慨括中、英雙方的談判,這便是一非人性化的過程。

五百萬人的命運,竟然決定於兩個巨人的角力之下,這是一宗荒唐的買賣。我們只是一部機器,一件貨品,有誰會理採我們的一聲異議? 我們的命運,不過是巨人手中的泥團!沒有尊嚴,沒有抉擇;有的,只是不斷的工作,不停地賺錢·香港人,不再是人,只是一的機器,一部不可以停止的機器,但有一天,當它

不再生產,不再賺錢的時候,天曉得我們的命運又會是如何!

今天,中、英雙方都在打「民意牌」,大家都聲稱自己掌握了香港人的「意願」,但說穿了,他們的所謂「香港人意願」,不過是哄人的騙局、棋局中的一步而已。到底他們考不考慮「民意」呢?他們是考慮的,但却並非如我們所想像中一般—————考慮全香港人的意願,他們所考慮的,是他們自身的利益,及一章掌握着香港經濟命脈的人的意願,在他們的眼中,我們不過是蟻民,沒有能力離開,只要社會中的反對壓力不會超越危險線,我們的意願便不會影響香港的安定繁榮。因此,為茗行政局的非官守議員可以「代表」香港人在英倫表示意見?為著中共要親吻香港的資本家?為何人大代表中沒有一些壓力團體的代表?為向港督尤德聲稱英國與港人意願沒有衝突?為著大家都聲稱尊重「港人意願」,但却聽問不到我們平民百姓這裡來?………………如此種種,都不難理解,大家所考慮的,不過是擁有經濟力量的「港人」的意願,我們這一幕蟻民,不過是人們脚底下的爛泥罷了!


國家、民族、掛在我們的口中,不過是一些空泛的名詞。我不相信、更加不會接受因為所謂「國家前途」、「民族利益」,便可以捐棄一己的尊嚴。我可以決定以「國家前途」、「民族利益」為自己的抉擇導向,但却不可以以此來強壓在他人頭上,人的尊嚴,乃建立於人可以為自己來決定、爭取自己的需要,沒有其他人可以代替他自己來決定。假若有的話,這便是壓迫。今天我們口中的民主,便是一個基於人的尊嚴而引申出來的概念,不是向在上位的高官來乞求,而是用自己底雙手來爭取。中、英雙方的意願,昭然若揭,英國方面,不過希望在香港易手後,仍然能繼續保持現有的利益:中國方面,則希望在九七年以後,能維持香港社會的安定繁榮。我們不應該再指望他們會為五百萬的香港人做一些甚麼,我們要甚麼,便要靠自己來爭取。中國、英國,都不過是希望在我們身上,撈一些油水;但真正的主人是我們,五百萬人團結在一起的力量是我們的武器。波蘭的人民告訴我們甚麼是爭取,除了自己,沒有人會為道義而考慮我們的意願。。在以後的日子中,我不相信我們可以影響談判的結果。我們亦不應該接受現時談判的結果。人民才是最後的裁決,我們爭取的,是五百萬港人自決香港的前途。我們親愛的大學生,難道一紙契約、一點「愛國」情操,比五百萬人的意願更為尊貴?

## English
(translated by Gemini)
**

### Five Million People in Hong Kong Determine Their Own Future

"Over himself, over his own body and mind, the individual is sovereign." — J.S. Mill

Regarding the issue of Hong Kong’s future, if one could summarize the Sino-British negotiations in a single phrase, it would be "a dehumanizing process."

The fate of five million people is being decided by the wrestling of two giants; this is an absurd transaction. We are merely machines, commodities—who will heed our voice of dissent? Our destiny is but a lump of clay in the hands of giants. There is no dignity, no choice; there is only constant work and the ceaseless making of money. Hong Kong people are no longer human, but machines that cannot stop. But one day, when these machines no longer produce or make money, heaven knows what our fate will be.

Today, both China and Britain are playing the "public opinion card," claiming to grasp the "will" of the Hong Kong people. But to put it bluntly, their so-called "will of the Hong Kong people" is nothing more than a deceptive trick—a mere move on a chessboard. Do they consider "public opinion"? They do, but not in the way we imagine. They do not consider the will of all Hong Kongers; rather, they consider their own interests and the will of those who hold the economic lifeblood of the city. In their eyes, we are but "ant-people" (commoners) with no power to leave. As long as the pressure of social opposition does not cross a dangerous line, our wishes will not affect Hong Kong’s stability and prosperity.

This explains why unofficial members of the Executive Council "represent" Hong Kong in London, why the CCP "kisses up" to Hong Kong capitalists, and why there are no representatives of pressure groups among the National People’s Congress. It explains why Governor Youde claims there is no conflict between Britain and the will of the people, and why everyone claims to respect that will while never actually listening to the common citizens. They only care about the "Hong Kong people" who possess economic power; the rest of us are just mud under their feet.

"Country" and "Nation" are just empty terms in our mouths. I do not believe, nor will I accept, that one’s dignity can be discarded for the sake of so-called "national future" or "national interest." I can choose to let these concepts guide my own decisions, but they cannot be forcibly imposed on others. Human dignity is built on the ability to decide and strive for one's own needs; no one else can decide for them. To do otherwise is oppression. The "democracy" we speak of today is a concept derived from human dignity—not something to be begged from high-ranking officials, but something to be seized with our own hands.

The intentions of both China and Britain are clear. Britain hopes to maintain its existing interests after the handover, while China hopes to maintain stability and prosperity after 1997. We should no longer expect them to do anything for the five million people of Hong Kong. What we want, we must fight for ourselves. Both are just looking to profit from us, but we are the true masters. The unity of five million people is our weapon. The people of Poland have shown us what it means to struggle; no one will consider our will out of morality except ourselves.

In the coming days, I do not believe we can influence the negotiation results, nor should we accept them. The people are the final judge. What we are fighting for is the right of five million Hong Kongers to determine their own future. To our dear university students: is a piece of paper or a bit of "patriotic" sentiment truly more precious than the will of five million people?
