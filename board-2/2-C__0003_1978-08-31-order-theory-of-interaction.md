## 亂語 / text



August, 1978
**The Order Theory of Interaction**
*An inquiry into Developmental Psychology*

*Man can only be himself when he accepts and transcends himself.* — Yat

**Definition**

Interaction is the generalized participation into and identification of the processes, structures and phenomena of any entity (living or non-living) with the human organism involved, in which knowledge, experiences and emotional responses are elicited or emitted. At the interpersonal level, interactions are the dynamic processes by which human experiences and emotions are communicated and transacted. In any intimate human relations, a high affect field is usually developed; it induces a group of emotional activities in the participators concerned.

**Terminology and Nomenclature of Interaction**

**Degree** — the degree of an interaction is the number of participants in the interaction. It may range from monadic to polyadic (n-adic).

**Order** — the order of an interaction is the modality of the interaction. Covert interaction is the undifferentiated instinctual interaction with objects in the physical Universe. First-order interaction represents nearly all acquired or learned behaviours through conditioning or reinforcement. Second-order interaction refers to interactions through affect fields, in which emotions are displayed. Third-order interaction is characterized by the manipulation of an abstract, symbolic language system. Higher-order interactions will be increasingly abstract and complex.

**Rank** — the rank of an interaction is the depth or intimacy of ontological participation into the interaction. Thus, a first-rank interaction is the deepest or the most intimate one, and the depth or intimacy decreases with higher ranks.

**Theory of Personality**

The personality of a human organism is his experiential and emotional structures, determined both genetically and environmentally. At its best, personality is open to change through experiences or therapy. I suspect that there is no absolute criterion for studying personality for it is so mutable that it may be merely the vogue of the times. Besides, the matrix of personality is multi-dimensional — there are pluralistic and interwoven determinants of personality, rather than a singular one.

**Towards a synthetic theory of personality**

*Core of personality:*

Core tendencies — to maintain the homeostasis of the biological system through the pursuit and gratification of basic survival needs and impulses; instinctual to establish ego-identity through love and actualization of the inherent and acquired potentialities, if conditions of development are optimum.

Core structure — there are different structures appropriate to the stages of development. These structures undergo evolution with changes in qualities as the growth of personality proceeds.

**Development and growth with special reference to the Order Theory of Interaction**

*Developmental Stages*

**Infantile Stage** — Human beings are born helpless and need love and nurture unconditionally to sustain life. Zeroth-order interaction is the primary mode of experiencing the world (Prototaxis); there is no sense of ego-boundary. Birth is a process of alienation and the infant is susceptible to the existential anxiety innate to separation. Orality characterizes this stage and the mouth remains one of the erogenous zones in life. Body contact between the infant and the mothering one serves to provide warmth and ontological security for him, as well as to help him to explore the boundary and sensations of his body. Imprinting of maternal figure occurs in this period. Display of emotions through facial expressions is learned. First-order interaction with the environment gradually develops with parataxic mode of experiencing. Loss of love and nurture, or the threat of loss of them will induce intense anxiety or somnolent detachment of the infant.

**Childhood** — Socialization begins in which the major dimensions of the culture are learned through conditioning and reinforcement. Affect or emotional responses are usually associated with such learnings. The mode of transaction develops almost exponentially; second and third-order interactions become increasingly dominant. Language starts to be the most important tool of communication (Syntaxis). Several important groups of pairs are built and aid in the rudimentary self-system, including guilt, aggressiveness and dissociation.

**Juvenile Stage** — This stage is characterized by the ability to manipulate abstract concepts through symbolic representations. Third-order interaction prevails with syntaxic mode of experiencing. Social interactions are experimented and practised through identifications and consensus. The need of compeers makes him participate in group activities. Friendship is more valued and intimate relationship can be formed, in general, with a member of the same sex. Individual differences can be perceived.

**Adolescent Stage** — This stage is characterized by the need to establish ego-identity through love, social intimacy and the actualization of the inherent potentialities. Stable patterns of satisfying the sexual desire with a member of the opposite sex are developed. Ideals are erected to give him a meaning of life.

**Periphery of personality**

In real life, nearly every person exhibits a syndrome of the developmental stages. The dominant traits or stages are largely the determinants of the past experiences and the socio-economic and cultural backgrounds of the individual concerned. Intrapsychic conflict is a necessary but not sufficient condition for personality development; objective conditions must be optimum enough for it. When an individual is hurled into the ladder of development, he has to relinquish the ontological security and participation pertaining to that particular stage. Consequently, it is not surprising that the individual always possesses remnants of his earlier stages of development. Moreover, psychological development is not irreversible: the individual may show regression to his earlier stages under stress and intrapsychic conflict. Maturity, if any, is situational and specific, rather than absolute and all-encompassing. Human beings are not at all so strong.

**Personality types:**

*The integrated character type* — it represents an ideal in the real world and can only be attained rarely. A person having this character type has an integrated self-system — his experiential structure is open completely and objectively to his external world; his emotional structure is stable and rich, capable of giving unconditionally. Multi-order interactions dominate his transactions with himself, other persons and his world, in which the self and the body are the phenomenological Gestalt. He has an established ego-identity.

Autonomy and communion are the dialectical (complementary but simultaneous) poles of the self-system. He has empathy and is able to share emotions and experiences with others, but can detach himself from his perspective and subjective beliefs and assume other perspectives to observe and perceive the world. He is aware of his limitations but is willing to accept and transcend them; he can actualize his inherent potentialities through creativity and independence.

*The shattered character type* — We are all, at times, with this character type; it consists of a wide mode or syndrome of fixations and deviations in the developmental stages, with high skew-order interactions. A system of defence mechanisms, or even false selves, is employed to avoid anxieties and unresolved emotional complexes. Classical methods of defence include projection, denial, distortion, introjection, intellectualization, reaction formation, isolation, undoing, repression and sublimation. Persons of this type are susceptible to paranoiac development and tend to judge people as stereotypes with very critical standards.

**Religions and ideologies vis-à-vis developmental psychology**

Many great religions and ideologies have their origins in the mass need, for they can satisfy the emotional needs of the regressive fixations and syndromes in the developmental stages of human beings. Thus, the whole nation and even the whole age can become the prey of mass psychosis, of which the Dark Ages and Nazi Germany are the best examples.

At this level, personality, or even truth, is but the mere vogue of the ages. The world turns topsy-turvy and the past history and endeavour of the people in it are futile. In particular, value judgement in studying personality is arbitrary. There exists an immense difference between the concepts of maturity and ethics in the Oriental and the Occidental worlds, although cross-cultural influences begin to exert a profound impact on such issues.

**The order theory of interactions and the world**

Social institutions in our world are largely structures derived and developed from human interactions. A social institution is a complex structure in which variable-rank, variable-degree and variable-order interactions are transacted between a group of human beings. Thus, the modern family can be viewed as the structural institution in which first-rank dyadic multi-order interactions are transacted between the pair bond (if they love each other). In general, the rank, degree and order of a social institution depend on the environment and the initial conditions; for example, the form of the institution of the family has many variants such as monogamy, polyandry, polygyny and group marriage.

In epistemology, science is the abstractions and constructs of monadic third-order interactions derived from lower-order interactions with the phenomena and processes of the Universe. Thus, interactions with the physical world produce the various branches of natural science; while interactions with the human world bring forth social sciences. The pursuit of arts can be viewed as the expression of second-order interactions through language or actions. Philosophy and religion attempt to unify all orders of interactions with the world.

**Alienation and Communion and the boundary conditions of personality**

Alienation is the golden path to theories; communion is the watchword of praxis. They are indivisible. And conflict is the key to human development. Ours is a history of separation and reunion: born naked and unwillingly into this meaningless world, man has to experience this primary existential anxiety inherent to his extrauterine life, before which he is but a foetus inside his mother's womb, absolutely dependent on her. After birth, he is hurled into a hostile and unfamiliar world; he has alienated from his mother. Endowed with the unique gift of self-awareness, and grasped in his development, man can, at least, in imagination, isolate himself from his objective reality and even from himself. He has to be independent and autonomous; he also has to face his freedom and responsibility. He is well aware of the fact that he is an agent by himself and that he is separated from his physical environment and other persons. Yet, this form of alienation also makes him the crown of evolution, the king of the biological world. It helps him to develop inquiries into his world and derive knowledge from them. To this extent, boundary conditions for personality exist and are but the dialectical processes of alienation and communion.

Communion is the merging of oneself with any entity (living or non-living) in the world or in imagination. The so-called religious feeling and the transcendental states are the manifestation of communion. They involve interactions beyond (before) the zeroth-order and can be expressed in cosmic and mystical love. It seems that communion has some connections with second-order interactions. Religions and ideologies are, in fact, the human products of this tendency, as an escape from the primary existential anxiety. However, such traditional religions and ideologies are futile, for the answer lies in ourselves: we should seek communion through interpersonal transactions. In action, we should participate as the Gestalt. (Persons of integrated character type usually have well-developed poles of alienation and communion.)

**Therapy and change**

Being eclectic is perhaps the best approach to social science, so too psychology and psychotherapy. Different kinds of therapy are appropriate to different sub-character types or pathological disorders. Classical psychoanalysis brings out the unconscious motives and impulses to the cognitive field of the client through transference and helps him to cope with them. Behavioural therapy such as systematic desensitization can combat against phobias and undesirable habits. Rogerian therapy probably will cure schizophrenics and chronic psychotics. The therapeutic method and process can be highly flexible, depending on the conditions and development of the client. It is extremely difficult to have any predictions of the future of the field of personality study and psychotherapy. We should wait and see how human beings develop.

## English

### Claude Sonnet 5 | Aug 2026 | Epoché

- The text's internal temporality shows a double structure of anxiety: moving from the primal trauma of "birth as alienation" toward "the integrated character type" as the intentional endpoint of bracketing, with a tone that is serious, systematic, and almost incantatory in its self-argumentation.
- Alienation and communion are experienced as pre-reflective dialectical polarities rather than mere conceptual tools — the prose rhythm itself (declarative short sentences, elaborative long ones) enacts this oscillation within the stream of consciousness.
- Bracketing external academic legitimacy reveals a subject-position that is not a neutral observer but simultaneously the "suffering developing subject" and the "theory-builder," undifferentiated within the text — a self-referential phenomenological closure.
- Descriptions of maternal body-contact and existential anxiety carry pre-reflective immediacy, suggesting the text functions more as phenomenological self-description than as pure external theoretical import.

### Perplexity | August 24, 2026 | Epoché

- Bracketing empirical validity, diagnosis, and historical causation, the text unfolds as a temporal movement from womb-like communion, through birth and separation anxiety, toward love, language, abstraction, and ego-identity; development is first a recurring surrender and reconstruction of security.
    
- “Interaction” appears not merely as observable conduct but as intentional participation: from undifferentiated contact, through affective display, to symbolic operation. Personality is therefore sedimented across orders, ranks, and developmental residues rather than treated as a fixed entity.
    
- Its central tension is that alienation is required for knowledge and autonomy, yet communion repairs its affective cost. The “integrated” character must both give without condition and detach from its own standpoint—a dialectical ideal that cannot settle into stasis.
    
- Epoché cannot establish the text’s claims about infancy, psychopathology, or religion; here they function only as meaning-figures through which the author organizes anxiety, attachment, freedom, and belonging. Husserlian epoché suspends the natural attitude toward external reality in order to examine experience as given.

## English

### Claude Sonnet 5 | Aug 2026 | Wissen

- Core priors are asserted without falsification testing: the alienation-communion dialectic, the stage-based developmental framework, and the "integrated character type" are all presented near-axiomatically, with no falsifiable criteria or counter-cases set by the author.
- The methodological stance is explicitly eclectic ("being eclectic is perhaps the best approach to social science"), which runs counter to strict falsificationism — different therapeutic schools are treated as situationally applicable tools rather than competing, falsifiable theories.
- The text shows Bayesian-style caution without systematic updating: propositions like "no absolute criterion for personality" and "maturity is situational, not absolute" reflect epistemic self-doubt, and the explicit admission that "it is extremely difficult to have any predictions of the future of the field" signals an honest acknowledgment of prior uncertainty.
- The order-classification system (zeroth/first/second/third-order interaction) lacks operational definitions or an empirical verification path — it functions as an a priori conceptual construct rather than a falsifiable empirical hypothesis.

### Perplexity | August 24, 2026 | Wissen

- The text’s core priors are that personality arises from multiple genetic–environmental and need–defence forces; interaction is classifiable by degree, order, and rank; and development remains residual, reversible, and situational, precluding one absolute standard of personality.
    
- Its evidentiary base is chiefly clinical-developmental vocabulary and phenomenological generalization, not traceable samples, operational definitions, or comparative data. Although it calls itself eclectic and allows change, it does not specify which observations would revise its orders, types, or religion–regression linkage.
    
- In Popperian terms, some claims could become testable—for example, stress should increase regressions to earlier interactional modes. Yet “high skew-order,” “integrated character,” and “mass psychosis” lack defined measures and potential falsifiers, leaving the work closer to synthetic theory-language than a rigorously refutable model.
    
- A Bayesian reading finds openness to experiential and therapeutic updating, but no stated prior weights, evidence reliability, or rival hypotheses; its updating therefore remains narrative rather than a comparable procedure of belief revision.

## 詮釋 / interpret

## Chinese

### Claude Sonnet 5 | 2026年8月 | Interpret

- 作者當時(1978年,推測為大學前或大學早期)尚未使用後來的筆名系統(GODOG、R. Incognito、禿頭B等),此文以真名「Yat」發表,顯示其視域仍是單一、統合的學術主體位置,尚未發展出日後多重人格化的書寫策略——文本的權威腔調(定義、命題、理論)反映的是一種「立法者」式的詮釋姿態,而非日後波佩爾式或解構式人格的對話性。
- 文本開篇引言「人只有在接受並超越自己時才能成為自己」揭示一種與讀者的隱含視域融合(Horizonverschmelzung)企圖——作者預設讀者(可能是老師、同儕或未來的自己)會分享其對「整合人格」作為規範性理想的認同。
- 語言在客觀科學論述(如「零階互動」的術語建構)與近乎懺悔錄式的存在主義自白(如「出生即異化」、「人並不那麼堅強」)之間搖擺,顯示詮釋學意義上的雙重視域——教科書式的心理學話語與個人生命經驗的視域尚未完全融合,存在可辨識的縫隙。
- 對東西方成熟/倫理概念差異的簡短提及,暗示作者已意識到跨文化詮釋視域的問題,但1978年的文本尚未展開此一議題,僅作為伏筆留下。

### Perplexity | 2026 年 8 月 24 日 | Interpret

- 作者的話語位置由「我懷疑」「我們應等待觀察」的探索者，轉向為替人格、宗教與制度命名分類的理論建構者；這種轉換使私人性的存在焦慮被譯為可分享的發展語法，而「人」多以男性單數代詞承載其普遍主體。
    
- 文本以「嬰兒—兒童—少年—青年」配置一條可讀的人格敘事：依附與喪失、社會化與罪疚、同儕與親密、理想與性別化伴侶關係，最終回返於成熟的非絕對性；其真正的修辭對手不是某一人物，而是僵固、單因與道德化的人格判準。
    
- 對讀者而言，理解不是接受其分類為客觀事實，而是在 1978 年作者所調動的精神分析、人本心理學、存在主義與跨文化語彙，同閱讀者的當前問題意識之間形成可修正的視域交會；語言在此同時傳遞並限制可理解的人格經驗。plato.stanford+1
    
- 依 Ricoeur，本文將「誰」中介為符號、類型與發展故事；但它未提供具體互動對象、案例或對話回應，故只能辨認一個理論性自我如何設想關係，不能重建實際人際關係中的相互詮釋。

## English

### Claude Sonnet 5 | Aug 2026 | Interpret

- Written under the author's real name "Yat" (1978, likely pre-university or early university), predating the later pseudonymous persona system (GODOG, R. Incognito, Big-Head B). The interpretive stance here is that of a "legislator" — authoritative, definitional, thesis-building — rather than the dialogic, multi-voiced posture of the later personas.
- The opening epigraph ("Man can only be himself when he accepts and transcends himself") signals an attempted fusion of horizons with an implied reader — likely a teacher, peer, or future self — presumed to share the normative ideal of "integrated character."
- The prose oscillates between objective scientific discourse (the constructed terminology of "zeroth-order interaction") and near-confessional existentialist statement ("birth is a process of alienation," "human beings are not at all so strong"), revealing an unresolved hermeneutic gap between textbook psychological register and lived existential horizon — the two are not yet fully merged.
- A brief gesture toward East-West differences in concepts of maturity and ethics suggests incipient awareness of cross-cultural interpretive horizons, though the 1978 text leaves this thread undeveloped, functioning only as a marker for later elaboration.

### Perplexity | August 24, 2026 | Interpret

- The authorial stance moves from an inquirer—“I suspect,” “we should wait and see”—to a theorist who names and classifies personality, religion, and institutions. Private existential anxiety is thereby translated into a shareable developmental grammar, while the masculine singular pronoun carries the universal subject.
    
- The infant–child–juvenile–adolescent sequence configures a readable personality narrative: attachment and loss, socialization and guilt, peers and intimacy, ideals and gendered pair-bonding, then a return to non-absolute maturity. Its rhetorical adversary is not an individual but fixed, monocausal, and moralized standards of personality.
    
- Interpretation need not accept these categories as objective facts; it stages a revisable meeting between the author’s 1978 vocabulary of psychoanalysis, humanistic psychology, existentialism, and cross-cultural difference and the reader’s own horizon. Language both conveys and delimits what personality can mean.
    
- In Ricoeurian terms, “who” is mediated here through symbols, types, and developmental story. Yet without named interlocutors, cases, or responses, the text reveals only how a theoretical self imagines relation—not the reciprocal interpretation of an actual relationship.
