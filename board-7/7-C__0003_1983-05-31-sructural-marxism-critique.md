### TRUCTURAL MARXISM: A HERESY

**柯爾梵**

生產（production）及實踐（practice）乃是結構馬克思主義的重要觀念。根據亞爾杜塞（Althusser）的分析，「實踐」是指透過S特定的勞動及生產方式把特定的原料轉化爲特定的產品之過程。換言之，「實踐」是在特定結構中勞動轉化的過度。人類的社會活動有四種生產：物質（material）、政治（political）、意識形態（ideological）及理論（theoretical）。這些生產都可以看作實踐（經濟、政治、意識形態及科學）。他認爲每一種生產都有其自主性及獨立的運作方式與邏輯，換句話說，**這些生產／實踐都不能互相還原**。它們具有相對自主性（relatively autonomous）及差異的歷史時間（differential historical time）。社會組成（social formation）的不同時刻（instances）不能還原爲獨特的本質。除了這些生產有不同的歷史規律、連續式不連續性之外，沒有一個共通的時間座標可以比較或量度這些生產。

亞爾杜塞爾認爲上述生產有（同質同形）（homologous）結構，每一種實踐包括了不同的原料、不同的生產關係及勞動。例如理論／科學實踐是透過理論勞動（理論的觀念及方法）和思想力量將觀念、表象……等轉化成知識。意識形態實踐將知覺及表象（社會組成中的成員和世界的關係）轉化。因爲不同範疇的實踐是移位的（dislocated）（不同生產有相對自立性及不能還原性（irreducibility）），所以知識的生產純屬於思想範疇，正如經濟生產是純屬經濟範疇一樣。套用他的三分類法：

- **普遍 I（Generality I）** —— 原料（物質、意識形態等）；

- **普遍 II（Generality II）** —— 勞動（勞動力、科學的方法／公理等）；

- **普遍 III（Generality III）** —— 產品（認知對象、商品等）。

### 分析架構

在科學/理論實踐中,香遍(原料)是前人實表活動遺留下來的思想及抽象觀念,透過普遍( 套科學的方法)問出普遍(科學觀念/知識)。因為知識的生產純屬於思想範疇,所以認知對象(object of knowledge)和實對象(real object) 能不相同,前者是在理論實踐中的一個理論對象,包含了一系列的觀念,後者則是認知過程的絕對參照點 (absolute reference point)

科學(或意識形態)的分析架構(problematic )是指將成本概念連繫及介定概念在關係系統中的位置、功能和本質的理論(或造形態)結構。分析架構規範了問題及其解決方法的可能性,分析架構中被忽略的某些概念及難題(「潛意識」結構)和明顯的輸述也是同等重要,而潛意識結構是要透過症狀式開 (symptomatic reading) (閱讀架構中不存在或不明顯的概念】才能被發現。亞爾杜塞爾認為分析架構是生產知識的決定因素,它們在結構上已經預設了某類認知對象及問題而忽略其它,所以閱讀者人類薑不是知識生產的主體。
### 認知缺裂
既然意識形他實踐不能給予人類「知識」(只有理論實踐才能得出知識)亞爾杜塞爾相信一套科學分析架構要從以前的意識形態分析架構分裂出來—————— 一個結構的異變,他稱之為認知缺裂(an epistemological break),整個過程是一個印証的危機,在這過程中,人類並不是立體,只是從屬於認勿缺裂的你用下。
根據亞爾社底顯的分析,理論實踐有著自己的季期及議定去介定產品的效度。
馬克思主義和唯心論/經驗論分析架構決裂。社會創成是一個複雜的整體結構,其範疇不能還原單的經濟論(economism)及機械論 (mechanism) 【所謂東唯物論〕。

### 超決定與結構因果論
社會田成是山特有的視雞體(政治、經濟及意識形態構成的綜合整體結構/超結構複雜體(Structural/superstructural complex)],亞社爾相信理論並不是超結構/上層建築,而斯科學會變成另一種的意識形態,失去其普遍效度,陷入知識社會學或歷史主義 historicism) 的謬誤。

超結構(政治、慈識形態)及結構(經濟基層建築、生產力及關係等)都有相對自主性,因為經濟生產並不能獨立地存在真空中,它的存在是決定於綜合整體。無論是結構或植結構時刻(instances)決定整體組合但又被整體組合決定,亞爾杜塞爾用特定的影響(specific effectivity)來形容這些超結構時刻。決定(determination)是变及多元的,在社會組成各層面的矛盾構成分層的影響。超決定 (overdetermination) 是指出超結構在最後時刻(Jant instance)是被經濟生產決定,但它們也具有相對自主性及特定之影響,不是基層建築(經濟生產)之按或純粹反射

經濟生產決定了其它生產的自主性/依賴性及特定影響。它也決定本身及其它生產是否制衡或非制面 (dominant or non-dominant),在歷史中每一個時刻,社會組成都有一個制衡結構(structure in dominance)革命的產生是有賴於當時的組合( conjuncture)(組合是指同層面的制衡關係,矛盾的凝聚及互相作用等),而不單是由經濟子看產生。 「歷史時間」的複雜性是取決於社會組成的複雜性。 所以一個「歷史過程」的橫切面是一個被超決定的瘦體組成之組合。

結構性因果論(structural causality)也是由這些分析而來,結構是蘊藏在其結果的原因,它是存在結果及其關係的整體,亞爾杜塞爾認為馬克思主義是理論上反對人道主義(anti-humanism因為實踐是結構組織,決定了結構中的元素,生產關係的結構分定及分配了其中的位置,角色及功能,結構馬克思主義的主體就是這是生產、政治及意識形態的關係 ,並不是所謂類別主體(category subject),歷史是一個沒有類別主體的過程(process without a subject),個體是不同實踐的產品
### 共時性結構分析/Synchronic Analysis
社會綜合整體(Complex Whole)可以視路共時性的接合結構(articulated structure),由相對及不可能的層面組成,巴里巴(Balibar」介定日一個生產模式(層面,是由以下三種元素及兩種關係所構成:

1. 生產者(勞動力)
2. 生產方法(勞動的對象及方法)
3. 车生產者分配剩餘勞動力
  A. 分配關係(生產過程)
  B.  資產關係(剝削過程)
  
套用比較分析(comparative analysis),上述五種觀念的組成可以介定生產模式。但生產模式的連橋/再生產可以退共時性分析去理解,一個生產模式會誘發介定這個生產模式的定義及關係,例如資本主我生產模式除了「生產」商品及剩餘價值外,還生產及永恆化了资本家和工人的社會關係,這個再生產過程介定了結構內的自主時間性及決定了資本流動。分配及消耗的過程,在最後時刻(last instance)決定了整個社會結構。

關於「結構馬克思主義」對意識形態的理解及其意識形態國家機器(ISA)和壓抑性國家機器( )等觀念在今期的學術時裝展已經討論過。至於結構馬克思主義對經驗主義、歷史主義、歷史目的論或自設立體論的批評,讀者可以參考《吳俊雄图》學苑第六期之「從歷史列非歷史」。
### 理論的死結
雖然亞爾杜塞爾批判了初驗主義及歷史主義、指出它們將思想和現實混淆,但他的分析卻帶着極重唯心主義色彩,他認為理論實踐有看自己的歷史和規律一換句話說:科學實踐得出的科學知識是客觀、普遍及絕對的,不受社會擊體或歷史所影響,所以他一否定生產過程及條件的歷史性,而又知道知識是受社會及歷史所影響,結果他認為科學實踐有相對自主但又受到社會影雪。可是他似乎未能闡明這些矛盾。

為甚麼結構馬克思主義是科學?它和其他科學有甚麼分別?科學和其他實踐活動又有甚麼關係?亞爾杜塞爾又憑甚麼來決定馬克思主義是個認知缺裂? 他的理論沒有一個給人明確不武斷的答覆。

既然馬克思主義的產生不是由工人運動而來,亞爾杜塞爾將理論實踐是原成一些獨立於政治及社會情祝的「生產」,結果「科學」甚至「造形態」的發展都和陪過矛盾及門爭無關。
根據他的分析,歷史是沒有必然性,革命或社會運動的產生是要視乎各相對自主層面的接合。低然歷史是偶然及不可預設的,馬克思主義變為一種具體分析社會的工具,但可以和其它「實踐」脫節。所以他被指為馬克思主義的異端。

結構因果論也流於太形而上及虛玄,亞爾杜塞爾的理論沒有提供令人滿意的答案。
### 方法論批評
根據亞爾杜塞爾的分析來看,我會認為他是一個唯理主義者(rationalist),試圖用一套系統(形面上的系統)來解釋政治及社會整體,但這類的大系統不免有選輯及驗的問題,為了要逃避這些難題,很多理論家都引入一些虚空的概念来挽救他們偉大的形而上學系統。
在亞爾杜塞爾的結構馬克思主義中,我們察覺到是充滿了術語及「嚴法」的定義,但這類的術語很容易團導其閱讀者,以為其內容及形式也是科學。

我認為亞爾杜塞爾只是提供了一套描繪政治社會的論述方法及思想方法,但若果要將這些分析方法提升為科學,就未免太武斷了。

Andre Glucksmann., A Ventriloguist Structuralism
Jorge Lorrain, The Concept of Ideology

### STRUCTURAL MARXISM: A HERESY

**柯爾梵**

Production and practice are central concepts in structural Marxism. According to Althusser’s analysis, “practice” refers to the process by which specific raw materials are transformed into specific products through specific forms of labour and modes of production. In other words, “practice” is a process of labour’s transformation within a particular structure.

Human social activity comprises four kinds of production: material, political, ideological, and theoretical. These forms of production may all be regarded as practices—economic, political, ideological, and scientific practice. Althusser argues that each kind of production possesses its own autonomy, mode of operation, and logic. Put differently, these productions/practices cannot be reduced to one another. They possess relative autonomy and differential historical times. The different instances of a social formation cannot be reduced to a single essence. Apart from the fact that these productions follow distinct historical laws and forms of discontinuous continuity, there is no common temporal coordinate by which they can be compared or measured.

Althusser maintains that these forms of production have homologous structures. Every practice includes distinct raw materials, relations of production, and forms of labour. For example, theoretical/scientific practice transforms ideas, representations, and so on into knowledge through theoretical labour—namely theoretical concepts and methods—and intellectual forces. Ideological practice transforms perceptions and representations: that is, the relation between members of a social formation and their world.

Because practices belonging to different domains are dislocated—because different forms of production are relatively autonomous and irreducible—knowledge production belongs exclusively to the domain of thought, just as economic production belongs exclusively to the economic domain. Using Althusser’s tripartite schema:

- **Generality I** — raw material: material objects, ideology, and so forth
    
- **Generality II** — labour: labour-power, scientific methods/axioms, and so on
    
- **Generality III** — product: objects of knowledge, commodities, and so forth
    

### The Problematic

In scientific or theoretical practice, Generality I—the raw material—consists of the ideas and abstract concepts inherited from previous practical activity. Through Generality II—the labour of scientific method—one produces Generality III: scientific concepts or knowledge.

Since the production of knowledge belongs exclusively to the domain of thought, the _object of knowledge_ need not be identical to the _real object_. The former is a theoretical object within theoretical practice and comprises a series of concepts; the latter is the absolute reference point of the process of knowing.

The problematic of science—or of ideology—refers to a theoretical, or ideological, structure that connects concepts and defines their positions, functions, and nature within a system of relations. A problematic governs both the possible questions and the possible means of resolving them. Certain concepts and difficulties that are omitted by the problematic—its “unconscious” structure—are as important as its explicit formulations. This unconscious structure can be discovered only through symptomatic reading: reading for concepts that are absent or not immediately visible within the framework.

Althusser considers the problematic to be the determining factor in the production of knowledge. Structurally, it already presupposes particular objects of knowledge and particular questions, while excluding others. Thus, the human reader is not the subject of knowledge production.

### Epistemological Break

Since ideological practice cannot give humanity “knowledge”—only theoretical practice can produce knowledge—Althusser believes that a scientific problematic must break away from an earlier ideological problematic. This is a structural mutation, which he calls an **epistemological break**. The entire process is a crisis of verification; in this process, human beings are not sovereign subjects, but merely subordinate to the operation of the epistemological break.

According to Althusser’s analysis, theoretical practice has its own history and its own criteria for determining the validity of its products.

Marxism breaks with the problematic of idealism and empiricism. A social formation is a complex totality whose categories cannot be reduced to simple economism or mechanism—the so-called crude materialism.

### Overdetermination and Structural Causality

A social formation is a distinctive complex whole: a structural/superstructural complex constituted by political, economic, and ideological instances. Althusser believes that theory is not itself part of the superstructure or the ideological “upper level”; otherwise science would become merely another ideology, lose its universal validity, and fall into the errors of the sociology of knowledge or historicism.

Both the superstructure—politics and ideology—and the structure—the economic base, productive forces, relations of production, and so forth—possess relative autonomy. Economic production cannot exist independently in a vacuum; its existence is determined by the complex whole. Structural and superstructural instances determine the totality while also being determined by it. Althusser uses the phrase **specific effectivity** to describe the efficacy of these superstructural instances.

Determination is multiple and complex. Contradictions at the various levels of a social formation produce stratified effects. **Overdetermination** means that, in the last instance, superstructural elements are determined by economic production; yet they retain relative autonomy and specific effects. They are not merely echoes or simple reflections of the base, that is, of economic production.

Economic production determines the autonomy/dependence and specific effects of other forms of production. It also determines whether it and the other forms of production are dominant or non-dominant. At every historical moment, a social formation has a **structure in dominance**.

The emergence of revolution depends upon the conjuncture of a given moment. A conjuncture refers to relations of dominance at the same level, the condensation and interaction of contradictions, and related conditions. Revolution is therefore not generated by the economy alone. The complexity of “historical time” depends upon the complexity of the social formation. A cross-section of a “historical process” is therefore a conjuncture of a complex whole that has been overdetermined.

Structural causality follows from these analyses. Structure is a cause immanent in its effects: it is the totality that exists through its effects and their relations. Althusser holds that Marxism is theoretically anti-humanist because practice is structurally organized and determines the elements within a structure. The structure of relations of production allocates positions, roles, and functions within it. The subject of structural Marxism is therefore the relation among economic production, politics, and ideology—not any supposed category-subject. History is a **process without a subject**, and individuals are products of different practices.

### Synchronic Structural Analysis

The complex social whole may be seen as a synchronically articulated structure, composed of relatively autonomous and irreducible levels. Balibar defines a mode of production—one such level—as consisting of three elements and two relations:

- Producers, or labour-power
    
- Means of production: the object and means of labour
    
- The allocation of surplus labour by non-producers
    
- Relations of distribution: the production process
    
- Relations of property: the process of exploitation
    

Through comparative analysis, the arrangement of these five concepts can define a mode of production. The articulation and reproduction of a mode of production may also be understood through synchronic analysis. A mode of production generates and reproduces the conditions and relations that define it.

For example, the capitalist mode of production produces not only commodities and surplus value; it also produces and perpetuates the social relation between capitalist and worker. This process of reproduction defines the structure’s autonomous temporality and determines the movement of capital. The processes of distribution and consumption determine the total social structure in the last instance.

Structural Marxism’s understanding of ideology, including its concepts of ideological state apparatuses (ISAs) and repressive state apparatuses, has already been discussed in the current issue of _Academic Fashion Show_. For structural Marxism’s criticism of empiricism, historicism, teleological views of history, and theories of the self-constituting subject, readers may consult “From Historicism to Non-Historicism” in Issue Six of _Xueyuan_, by Ng Chun-hung.

### Theoretical Impasses

Although Althusser criticizes empiricism and historicism for confusing thought with reality, his own analysis carries a strong idealist tendency. He argues that theoretical practice has its own history and laws. In other words, scientific knowledge produced by scientific practice is objective, universal, and absolute, unaffected by the social totality or by history.

He thus denies the historicity of the conditions and processes of production, while also recognizing that knowledge is affected by society and history. As a result, he holds that scientific practice is relatively autonomous but nevertheless subject to social influence. Yet he seems unable to clarify these contradictions.

Why is structural Marxism a science? How does it differ from other sciences? What relation does science have to other practical activities? On what grounds does Althusser determine that Marxism constitutes an epistemological break? His theory offers no clear and non-arbitrary answer.

Since Marxism’s emergence is not understood as arising from the workers’ movement, Althusser treats theoretical practice as a form of “production” independent of political and social circumstances. The development of “science,” and even of ideology, consequently becomes detached from past contradictions and struggles.

According to his analysis, history has no necessity. The emergence of revolution or social movements depends upon the articulation of relatively autonomous levels. Since history is contingent and cannot be predetermined, Marxism becomes an instrument for the concrete analysis of society—but one that may become detached from other “practices.” This is why Althusser has been described as a heretic within Marxism.

Structural causality, too, becomes excessively metaphysical and abstruse. Althusser’s theory does not provide a satisfactory answer.

### Methodological Critique

On the basis of Althusser’s analysis, I would regard him as a rationalist: someone attempting to explain the political and social totality through a system—indeed, a formal system. But grand systems of this kind inevitably encounter logical and empirical problems. In order to evade such difficulties, many theorists introduce empty concepts to rescue their grand metaphysical systems.

In Althusser’s structural Marxism, one finds an abundance of terminology and rigorously defined concepts. Yet this kind of terminology can easily mislead readers into assuming that the content, as well as the form, is scientific.

I believe that Althusser offers only a mode of discourse and a way of thinking through which to describe political society. To elevate these analytical methods into a science, however, is excessively arbitrary.

### References

- Norman Geras, _Althusser’s Marxism: An Assessment_
    
- André Glucksmann, _A Ventriloquist Structuralism_
    
- Jorge Larrain, _The Concept of Ideology_
