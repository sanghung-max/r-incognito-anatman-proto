## 觀景 vista

Out of this relatively dry and non-controversial essay on the observation of a publicized astronomical event, the use of the pseudonym "朱咀" was deliberate.   It was the nickname of the previous editor-in-chief of 「學苑」(currently a renowned sociology professor in Hong King Post-Secondary Institutions).  The choice reflected  "無我"'s mockery and distaste of the dominant subculture produced and reproduced in 「學苑」from "academic stars" with contemporary sociological background.  I hijacked his nickname and published an essay based on using scientific method and observation in practice (or praxis), in complete contradiction with the type of persona he projected.

The unexpected observation made by the LLMs for this piece was the association of the scribbled "affective" messages to call for participation in 「學苑」 was coincidental.  In retrospect from their association, I would have intentionally done the same.

### 九星連珠 觀測法

**朱咀**

觀測九星連珠的方法,主要是用目 視及攝影記錄,目視觀測可用普通的雙 筒望遠鏡(例如7×50或8×30)或天 文望遠鏡,甚至可用肉眼測定各個行星 的位置,估計它們的光度。每隔一段時 間(如三天)觀察一次,將結果記在一 份星圖(最好是六等或以上)便可。這 些研究,都可以幫助了解這些行星的位 置變化。 讀者亦可以用攝影方法記錄九星連 珠的現象,用一部普通相機及一座腳架 便可,菲林可試用四百度的高速菲林, 用一百六十度或四百度的幻燈片也可, 拍攝的地點要遠離城市燈光,距離調至 無限遠,光圈較至最大,快門利用B檔 ,使用失打繩曝光十五至二十秒便可, 若讀者知道如何冲晒菲林,那更加方便 ,可以使用强迫顯影方法,使曝光時間 減至十至十五秒,在相片放大時便不會 有星流跡出現。讀者也可以使用較慢速 的菲林,曝光數分鐘甚至一兩小時,拍 攝「星流跡」,利用一些樹木及建築物 做前景,更可使到作品成為一幅藝術照 片。如果讀者擁有天文望遠鏡,可用追 蹤方法作較長時間曝光。因為是次九星 連珠的分佈角度很大,用二十mm的廣 角鏡也不能一次拍攝。用標準鏡頭可將 行星的位置分成三四組拍攝。

### 九大行星的位置

一、水星一三月底,四月上旬水星接近 太陽,不能觀測。四月下旬以後,水星成為昏星,傍晚出現在西南天空,亮度 約-1.0等。六月廿六日西大距,西大 距前後,水星成為晨星,日出前從東方 升起,較易觀測。以後數月水星的高度 較低,所以較難觀測。 

二、金星一一月底至十月為晨星,四月 距太陽 46°,星等-4.0等,五、六、 七、八月由雙魚座經金牛座雙子座到巨 蟹座,亮度也逐漸變暗。十一月中旬以 後,金星為昏星,至十二月,金星約在 日落下約一小時後落入地平線。 

三、火星一五、六、七月在大陽落山後 前半夜在西南方天空,八、九月由室女 座經天秤座到天蠍座,難於觀測。 

四、木星一四月份晚七點出地平,以後 升起時間逐漸提早,七月份日落不久, 木星在正南方天空,前半夜一直可見, 易於觀測。十一、十二月在大陽後面, 看不到木星。 

五、土星一四月至六月在日沒時,出現 在東南天空,大半夜都可見到。七月至 八月中旬,土星可見時間約四小時,九 月份在西南方天空,約二小時即沒入地 平線。九月以後在太陽另一面,較難觀 測。 

六、天王星、海王星和冥王星一它們距 離比較遠,用肉眼很難直接看到。用雙 筒或小型望遠鏡可以看到天王星和海王 星。 

五月十六日九大行星再次聚集於 的扇形內,讀者可以把握時間,透 過目視及攝影觀測,研究一下難得一見 的「九星連珠」。

translated by Copilot Sep 2026

Here is a **full, pragmatically faithful English translation** of _九星連珠觀測法_ (“Methods for Observing the Nine‑Planet Conjunction”), preserving the tone, clarity, and practical, amateur‑astronomy style of the original.

### **Methods for Observing the Nine‑Planet Conjunction**

**朱咀**

### **Visual Observation**

The primary methods for observing the nine‑planet conjunction are **visual observation** and **photographic recording**. For visual observation, you may use an ordinary pair of binoculars (such as 7×50 or 8×30), a small astronomical telescope, or even the naked eye to determine the positions of the planets and estimate their brightness. Observe once every few days (for example, every three days), and record the results on a star chart (preferably one that goes down to magnitude 6 or fainter). Such observations help you understand how the positions of the planets change over time.

### **Photographic Observation**

Readers may also record the conjunction using photography. A simple camera and a tripod are sufficient. You can try ISO‑400 high‑speed film, or 160‑degree or 400‑degree slide film. Choose a shooting location far from city lights. Set the focus to infinity, open the aperture fully, and use the **B‑setting** on the shutter. With a cable release, expose for **15 to 20 seconds**.

If you know how to develop film yourself, even better — you can use **forced development** to reduce exposure time to **10 to 15 seconds**, preventing star trails when enlarging the photograph.

You may also use slower film and expose for several minutes or even one to two hours to capture **star trails**. Using trees or buildings as foreground elements can turn the result into an artistic photograph.

If you have an astronomical telescope, you can use **tracking** for longer exposures. Because the angular spread of this nine‑planet conjunction is quite large, even a 20 mm wide‑angle lens cannot capture all planets in a single shot. Using a standard lens, you can divide the planets into three or four groups and photograph them separately.

### **Positions of the Nine Planets**

### **1. Mercury**

In late March and early April, Mercury is close to the Sun and cannot be observed. After late April, Mercury becomes an evening star, appearing in the southwestern sky at dusk, with a brightness of about –1.0 magnitude. On June 26 it reaches greatest western elongation; around this time Mercury becomes a morning star, rising in the east before sunrise and easier to observe. In the following months Mercury remains low in the sky and is harder to see.

### **2. Venus**

From late January to October, Venus is a morning star. In April it is 46° from the Sun, at –4.0 magnitude. In May, June, July, and August it moves from Pisces through Taurus and Gemini to Cancer, gradually dimming. After mid‑November, Venus becomes an evening star; by December it sets about an hour after sunset.

### **3. Mars**

In May, June, and July, Mars appears in the southwestern sky during the first half of the night after sunset. In August and September it moves from Virgo through Libra to Scorpius and becomes difficult to observe.

### **4. Jupiter**

In April, Jupiter rises around 7 p.m. and rises earlier each day thereafter. In July, shortly after sunset, Jupiter is already in the southern sky and remains visible throughout the first half of the night — easy to observe. In November and December it is behind the Sun and cannot be seen.

### **5. Saturn**

From April to June, Saturn appears in the southeastern sky at sunset and remains visible for most of the night. From July to mid‑August, Saturn is visible for about four hours. In September it appears in the southwestern sky and sets after about two hours. After September it is on the far side of the Sun and becomes difficult to observe.

### **6. Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto**

These planets are much farther away and cannot be seen with the naked eye. With binoculars or a small telescope, Uranus and Neptune can be observed.

### **The Conjunction Event**

On **May 16**, the nine planets once again gather within a fan‑shaped region of the sky. Readers should seize the opportunity to observe this rare “nine‑planet conjunction” through visual and photographic methods.
