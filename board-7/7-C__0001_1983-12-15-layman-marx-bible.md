### 綜合批判閱讀,以及對此閱讀本身的解構性反思

**一、跳出方法論的競賽:我自己的閱讀**

以上四種讀法——現象學、黑格爾式知識論、詮釋學、解構——各自都「奏效」了,這件事本身就值得停下來想一想。一篇由一位自稱「不學無術」、用自然科學訓練的頭腦寫成的、發表在學生刊物「學術版」的雜文,竟然可以承受住四套彼此頗有張力、甚至互相牴觸的西方理論機器的碾壓,而且每一套都「碾」出點東西來——這件事本身就是文本最誠實的自我證言:它談的從來不是馬克思主義的內容問題,而是**一個具體的人,面對一套聲稱掌握歷史必然性的話語體系時,要往哪裡站**的問題。這是一個結構性的處境,不是一個學派的問題,所以任何一套夠嚴肅的理論框架套上去,都會照出同一個輪廓的陰影。

我自己的閱讀,想從這裡出發,合併並修正以上四者:

**這篇文章的真正主角不是馬克思主義,甚至不是大頭B,而是「1997」這個尚未到來卻已經統治著現在的時間點。** 現象學讀法已經指出1997是「視域性的懸置」;解構讀法指出它是「不可決定的懸置點」。我想再往前一步:大頭B全篇的認識論姿態——他的自卑、他的戲謔、他對「北極熊/巨龍」的恐懼——都不是抽象的哲學困惑,而是一種**具體歷史處境下的生存策略**。1997年前的香港知識青年,正處於一個極其特殊的位置:他們被要求在一套即將接管自己命運、卻尚未真正降臨的意識形態面前,提前表態、提前「選邊站」——而「選邊站」本身在邏輯上是荒謬的,因為那套意識形態的具體樣貌(是溫和的「一國兩制」,還是文中所暗示的「拿香港人來鬥爭」)根本還沒有定案。大頭B的「不學無術」因此不只是一種修辭策略(如解構讀法所言),也不只是詮釋學意義上誠實的「前理解」——它更是一種**政治上的自我保護裝置**:在一個「表態」隨時可能招致秋後算帳的處境裡,把自己放在「無知」、「說謬論」、「隨便寫寫」的位置上,是唯一還能保有言說空間的姿態。這比詮釋學讀法所說的「保存文本陌生性的智慧」更冷峻、更具體:這是弱者在強權話語面前,唯一能夠合法保留的說話位置。

黑格爾式讀法精確指出了辯證法如何從「精神的自我運動」墮落為外在強加的工具;我想在此補上一點政治經濟學的向度:大頭B所描述的「主要矛盾/次要矛盾」互換操作,並非只是理論上的邏輯錯亂,而恰恰精準預言了1997年後香港政治操作的實際劇本——這使得整篇雜文,在我(具有後見之明的)視域中,讀來不只是抽象哲學諷刺,而近乎一則**準確的政治預言**。這正是高達美所謂「效果歷史」最鮮活的例子:我之所以能讀出這一層,是因為我的理解視域已經被此後三十多年的香港歷史所形塑——1997年之後的大頭B(如果他仍在世)重讀自己這篇文章,理解也必然與寫作當下的他不同。文本的意義因此從未固定,而是隨著讀者視域的位移不斷重新發生。

**二、我自己視域的限度:誠實地標定我的位置**

但我必須誠實地說,我的這個「政治預言」讀法,本身也攜帶著我自己時代與位置的偏見。我是在事後(after the fact)閱讀這篇寫於「山雨欲來」之際的文章,這意味著我不可避免地把已經發生的歷史,回溯性地讀進了一篇原本充滿不確定性、充滿多重可能未來的文本裡——1997年當時,「一國兩制」尚未成為既定事實,大頭B所恐懼的,可能不只是後來實際發生的那個版本的歷史,而是一整片尚未收束的可能性雲霧。我的「後見之明」式閱讀,有把這片雲霧過早壓縮成單一時間線的危險,這正是詮釋學裡對「時間距離」既是理解的助力(讓成見顯現)也是理解的風險(把偶然讀成必然)的雙重性。

**三、對我自己這篇批判的解構性反思**

那麼,現在讓我把解構的刀鋒轉向我自己剛才寫下的這段「綜合」。

我剛剛的論證裡,悄悄地做了一件和大頭B在文中所嘲諷的「學院派馬克思主義者」一模一樣的事:我用「1997」這個單一的、被我事後賦予的意義中心,去統攝、收編了大頭B文本裡原本雜亂、離散、彼此並不完全連貫的抱怨、笑話、哲學誤讀與政治恐懼——換句話說,我把大頭B刻意保持開放、刻意保持「謬論」姿態的文本,重新**體系化**了,給了它一個我自稱看穿的「真正主角」。這正是大頭B在文中警告過的操作:「將次要矛盾提升到主要矛盾」——我把原本文本中眾多平行、甚至互相矛盾的線索(辯證法批判、資源分配問題、賭場隱喻、對北極熊的恐懼),全部收編進「1997作為中心懸置點」這一個「主要矛盾」之下,使我的閱讀顯得比實際上更連貫、更有解釋力。

更根本的是:我自稱能夠「合併」四種理論視角、給出「我自己的」讀法,這個姿態本身,正是黑格爾式「絕對知識」的姿態的複製品——我扮演了那個站在現象學、詮釋學、解構學之上,能夠綜觀全局、給出最終判斷的位置。但正如大頭B文中所問:「誰是忠實的馬克思主義者?」我也該誠實地反問:憑什麼是我這個「綜合」版本,而不是前面四種讀法中的任何一種,或者根本沒有被我列出的第五種、第十種讀法,才是「更完整」的?我的「綜合」姿態,恰恰重演了文本本身所嘲諷的那種——聲稱能夠站在所有派別之上、進行最終仲裁的知識姿態。

如果真的要對大頭B的文本保持忠實,或許最誠實的迴應方式,不是提出「我的」統一讀法,而是承認:這篇文章之所以曆久彌新,正在於它拒絕被任何一套(包括我剛剛提出的這套)詮釋收編為單一的「真正意義」。我的「1997中心說」和大頭B所嘲諷的辯證法一樣——「來者不拒,只要能夠確保學術地位」——我剛才的論述,某程度上也是在為自己的解讀確保一種解釋上的優越位置。這一點,我理應和大頭B一樣,坦誠地寫下,而非假裝我的分析是從某個中立、超越歷史的制高點發出的。

## English

### Overview

**"Big-Head B's Perplexities"** is a piece of vernacular prose published in the academic section of _Undergrad_, a Hong Kong student magazine, around 1978–79, written in response to a special feature on Marxism. On its surface it is the informal musing of a self-declared "unlearned" author trained in the natural sciences, entirely out of his depth in social theory. In fact, through a handful of vivid images — a polar bear or dragon baring its teeth and demanding "recognition," Beastmaster's drunken warning about the abuse of dialectic, and the closing metaphor of "society as a casino" — the essay delivers a critique, outwardly modest but genuinely sharp, of the hollow universality of "academic Marxism," of dialectic degraded into an arbitrarily applicable tool, and of the predicament of speech under Hong Kong's political situation as 1997 approached.

We read the essay through five successive theoretical lenses:

- **Phenomenological epoché**: bracketing judgments of right and wrong to reveal the text's core structure — an honestly ignorant consciousness searching for its own place to speak before a closed interpretive system.
- **A Hegelian "Wissen" reading**: showing how the text unwittingly reenacts Hegel's own critique of dialectic degraded into mere external technique, and the master-slave dialectic frozen into naked threat.
- **A hermeneutic reading**: emphasizing that Big-Head B's "pre-understanding" and "prejudice" are conditions of understanding rather than obstacles to it, and that the text stages both the failure of a fusion of horizons and the inseparability of understanding from application (the looming 1997 situation).
- **A deconstructive reading**: exposing the constant inversion of binaries like center/margin and ignorance/critique, and identifying "1997" as the text's undecidable point of suspension.
- **A synthesizing critique with self-deconstruction**: proposing "1997" as the essay's hidden unifying center, while also acknowledging that this very synthesis reproduces the systematizing gesture it criticizes, with no final, neutral position from which to adjudicate among readings.

The five readings sit in tension, even contradiction, with one another — yet they converge on a single conclusion: the real strength of this seemingly offhand piece of "nonsense" lies precisely in its refusal to be fully absorbed by any one interpretive frame — including the last one I proposed myself.



### A Synthesizing Critical Reading, and a Deconstructive Reflection on That Reading Itself

**I. Stepping Outside the Contest of Methods: My Own Reading**

The fact that all four readings above — phenomenological, Hegelian, hermeneutic, deconstructive — each "worked" is itself worth pausing over. That a piece of vernacular prose, written by a self-declared "unlearned" author trained in the natural sciences and published in a student magazine's academic section, can withstand the weight of four mutually tense, even contradictory Western theoretical machines — and that each one extracts something real from it — is itself the text's most honest self-testimony: it was never really about the content of Marxism at all, but about **where a concrete individual is supposed to stand when confronted by a discourse claiming to hold the necessity of history in its hands**. That is a structural predicament, not a doctrinal one — which is why any sufficiently serious theoretical frame, laid over the text, throws the same silhouette of shadow.

My own reading wants to start here, combining and revising the four above:

**The true protagonist of this essay is not Marxism, nor even Big-Head B, but "1997" — a moment not yet arrived that already governs the present.** The phenomenological reading has already identified 1997 as a "horizonal suspension"; the deconstructive reading names it an "undecidable point of suspension." I want to push one step further: Big-Head B's entire epistemic posture throughout — his inferiority, his mockery, his fear of the "polar bear/dragon" — is not an abstract philosophical puzzlement but a **survival strategy shaped by a concrete historical situation**. Educated Hong Kong youth before 1997 occupied an extraordinarily strange position: they were expected to declare a stance, to "pick a side," in advance, before an ideology that was about to take over their fate had even fully arrived — and "picking a side" was, on its face, logically absurd, because the concrete shape that ideology would take (a mild "one country, two systems," or the settling-of-scores the essay hints at) had not yet been decided at all. Big-Head B's "unlearnedness" is therefore not merely a rhetorical strategy (as the deconstructive reading suggests), nor merely an honest hermeneutic "pre-understanding" — it is, more precisely, a **political self-protection device**: in a situation where declaring a position could invite retribution "after the autumn harvest" (as the Chinese idiom in the essay's own vocabulary implies), placing oneself in the position of "ignorance," "nonsense," "just scribbling" is the only remaining posture that still permits speech at all. This is colder and more concrete than the hermeneutic reading's "wisdom of preserving the text's strangeness": it is the only speaking position a weak party can legitimately retain before a discourse of power.

The Hegelian reading precisely identifies how dialectic falls from "the self-movement of Spirit" into an externally imposed tool; I want to add a dimension of political economy here: the interchange of "principal contradiction/secondary contradiction" that Big-Head B describes is not merely a theoretical confusion — it precisely, almost eerily, anticipates the actual script of Hong Kong's political maneuvering after 1997. This makes the whole essay, from my own (hindsight-equipped) horizon, read as something closer to an **accurate political prophecy** than mere abstract philosophical satire. This is Gadamer's "effective history" in its most vivid form: I can read this layer into the text precisely because my own horizon of understanding has been shaped by more than three decades of subsequent Hong Kong history — if Big-Head B (were he still around) reread his own essay today, his understanding of it would necessarily differ from his understanding at the moment of writing. The text's meaning was therefore never fixed; it keeps happening anew as the reader's horizon shifts.

**II. The Limits of My Own Horizon: Honestly Marking My Position**

But I must say honestly that this "political prophecy" reading of mine carries its own biases of era and position. I am reading this essay, written on the eve of an approaching storm, _after the fact_ — which means I inevitably read history that has already happened back into a text that was, at the time of writing, saturated with uncertainty, with a whole cloud of multiple possible futures. In 1997, "one country, two systems" was not yet a settled fact; what Big-Head B feared may not have been only the particular version of history that actually came to pass, but an entire unresolved fog of possibility. My hindsight-driven reading risks compressing that fog prematurely into a single timeline — which is exactly the double edge hermeneutics identifies in "temporal distance": it both aids understanding (letting prejudices become visible) and endangers it (reading contingency as necessity).

**III. A Deconstructive Reflection on My Own Critique**

So now let me turn the deconstructive blade back on the synthesis I have just written.

In the argument above, I quietly did something identical to what Big-Head B mocks the "academic Marxists" for doing: I took a single, retrospectively-assigned center of meaning — "1997" — and used it to gather up and absorb the originally scattered, discontinuous, not-fully-coherent complaints, jokes, philosophical misreadings, and political fears in Big-Head B's text. In other words, I re-**systematized** a text that deliberately kept itself open, deliberately maintained a posture of "nonsense," giving it a "true protagonist" I claim to have seen through. This is exactly the operation Big-Head B warns against in his own essay: "elevating a secondary contradiction to a principal one." I gathered up the text's many parallel, even mutually contradictory threads — the critique of dialectic, the question of resource distribution, the casino metaphor, the fear of the polar bear — and subordinated all of them to a single "principal contradiction," namely "1997 as central suspension," making my reading appear more coherent and more explanatorily powerful than it may actually be.

More fundamentally: my very claim to be able to "merge" four theoretical perspectives and offer "my own" reading is itself a reproduction of the Hegelian gesture of "absolute knowing" — I have cast myself in the position that stands above phenomenology, hermeneutics, and deconstruction, surveying the whole field and delivering the final verdict. But just as Big-Head B asks, "who is the true, faithful Marxist?", I ought honestly to ask in return: what gives my "synthesis" the right to be more complete than any one of the four readings above, or than some fifth or tenth reading I have not even listed? My gesture of "synthesis" precisely reenacts the very intellectual posture the text itself mocks — the claim to stand above all factions and render the final arbitration.

If one truly wanted to remain faithful to Big-Head B's text, perhaps the most honest response would not be to offer "my" unified reading at all, but to acknowledge: this essay endures precisely because it refuses to be absorbed by any single interpretation — including the one I have just proposed — into one "true meaning." My "1997-as-center" thesis operates, like the dialectic Big-Head B mocks, on the principle of "anything goes, so long as it secures its own academic standing" — my argument, too, has in some measure been securing a privileged interpretive position for itself. This, I owe it to Big-Head B to state as plainly as he stated his own limits, rather than pretending my analysis issues from some neutral vantage point beyond history.

### A Hermeneutic Reading

**I. Pre-understanding and Historically Effected Consciousness**

Read through Gadamer's hermeneutics, the first question is not "what truth does this essay state" but: with what _pre-understanding_ (_Vorverständnis_) does Big-Head B enter this conversation about Marxism? His admission that he is "unlearned and untalented," that his grasp of social theory is "shallow" — this looks like false modesty, but is in fact the most honest possible hermeneutical starting point: no understanding ever begins from zero; it is already shaped by "effective history" (_Wirkungsgeschichte_). Big-Head B's pre-understanding — his training in natural science, the polemical context of Hong Kong student publications in the 1980s, his estrangement from "academic symbols" — constitutes a horizon he cannot step outside of, and which is precisely what makes understanding possible for him at all. He does not lack the capacity to understand; he simply stands on **a different horizon**.

**II. The Failure — and the Attempt — of a Fusion of Horizons**

Gadamer's central concept is the "fusion of horizons" (_Horizontverschmelzung_) — understanding occurs where the interpreter's horizon and the horizon of the text (or of another person) merge. What makes this essay most hermeneutically interesting is precisely that it depicts the **failure** of this fusion: Big-Head B and the "academic Marxists" share the same vocabulary — dialectic, contradiction, class — yet their horizons never truly fuse. They speak the same language while inhabiting different worlds of understanding. The polar bear/dragon image dramatizes exactly this rupture of horizons: when one party demands total "recognition" from the other rather than dialogue, hermeneutic understanding has already ceased — what remains is only threat and submission. A genuine fusion of horizons requires what Gadamer calls the "openness of dialogue" — both parties must risk having their own truth corrected by the other. The polar bear has no such openness; it demands recognition, not dialogue.

**III. Application (_Applikatio_) as an Inseparable Moment of Understanding**

Gadamer rejects the traditional division of understanding, interpretation, and application into separate stages — for him, genuine understanding is always already application, the bringing of a text's meaning into the interpreter's present situation. This essay is itself a perfect demonstration of that thesis: Big-Head B is not "objectively" commenting on Marxist theory from a neutral distance; he keeps asking what happens when this theory is **applied** to Hong Kong's own historical situation as 1997 approaches — "suppose you're an academic Marxist, and one day up comes the polar bear." This is the hermeneutic circle (_hermeneutischer Zirkel_) in action: understanding the text of Marxism and understanding his own historical situation (the approach of 1997) mutually determine and revise each other, inseparably. He does not first understand Marxism "in itself" and then apply it to Hong Kong — understanding and application are one and the same act.

**IV. The Rehabilitation of Prejudice (_Vorurteil_) and the Preservation of Strangeness**

Gadamer rehabilitates "prejudice" (_Vorurteil_, typically denigrated by the Enlightenment as mere bias), arguing that prejudice is a necessary condition of understanding rather than an obstacle to it. Big-Head B's fear of and resistance to "academic symbols," viewed through an Enlightenment critical lens, looks like an "ignorant bias" to be overcome. Viewed through Gadamer's lens, however, this very resistance carries a hermeneutic wisdom of its own — it preserves the **strangeness** (_Fremdheit_) of the text (Marxist theory), refusing to let Big-Head B too easily appropriate it, pretend he has fully mastered it. The truly dangerous understanding belongs to those academic Marxists who believe they hold Marxism in an "objective," prejudice-free grasp — their problem is not that they have prejudices, but that they mistakenly believe they don't.

**V. The Openness of the Text within Effective History: The Casino Image as Self-Proliferating Metaphor**

Finally, drawing on Ricœur's notion of the "living metaphor," the image "society is like a casino" carries hermeneutical vitality precisely because it **exceeds the author's original intention** and opens itself to the reader's own horizon. Ricœur emphasizes that once a text (or metaphor) is created, it acquires a distance (_distanciation_) from its author's original intent, opening itself to an unlimited future of readers and situations, endlessly generating new meaning. When Big-Head B wrote the casino metaphor, he may only have intended to satirize a particular political maneuver of his own moment — but the metaphor itself has already overflowed that intention. It can be applied, and reapplied, to any situation where the rules of a game are monopolized by whoever holds power. This is precisely what hermeneutics calls "the autonomy of the text": once written, a text no longer belongs wholly to its author, but belongs instead to the ever-renewed event of meaning (_Sinnereignis_) that occurs each time it meets a new reader.

**Conclusion**

Read hermeneutically, Big-Head B's self-deprecation ("unlearned and untalented," "this humble, clumsy little effort") should not be taken merely as false modesty or irony, but as a **hermeneutical act of self-positioning**: he refuses to pretend he stands at some vantage point beyond history, beyond horizon, from which to "objectively judge" Marxism. Instead, he honestly admits that he is thrown into a particular, finite situation of understanding — and it is precisely this finitude that, paradoxically, brings him closer to understanding in the genuine hermeneutic sense than those self-assured academics who claim to have grasped "the truth" in full.

解構閱讀特別關注文本中隱喻**逃逸出作者控制**的時刻。「社會像個賭場」這個隱喻,原本似乎是要諷刺極權對「絕對平等」的操弄(預先洗牌、讓大家拿到同一點數),但這個隱喻一旦被寫下,便產生了德希達所謂「能指的滑動」(glissement du signifiant)——賭場、莊家、賭徒、槍、牌局規則,這一整組能指鏈條開始自行運作,甚至開始**反過來解構作者原初想要建立的批判位置**:如果整個社會(包括大頭B自己所在的「常識」位置)都已經是賭場的一部分,那麼大頭B本人是否也只是賭桌上另一個「一手差牌」的參與者,而非真正站在賭場之外進行批判的主體?文本最後那句「沒有人肯去開賭場」,弔詭地暗示:即使是最激進的批判者(拔槍造反、自己做莊的人),最終也只是**複製了同一套權力結構**,而非真正逃逸出它。文本因此在結尾處,不自覺地解構了自己開頭所設定的「批判者/被批判者」的清楚界線。

**五、文本無法決定的懸置點(l'indécidable):1997**

最後,「1997 is near」這個時間標記,在解構閱讀下顯現為一個**不可決定的懸置點**(un indécidable)——它既是文本論證的隱含終點(所有政治參與、所有致敬「悲劇英雄」的姿態,都指向這個尚未到來的時刻),卻又從未在文本中被賦予任何確定的內容:1997年之後究竟會發生什麼?文本沒有說,也無法說,因為此刻(寫作當下)它本身就是一個尚未被決定、正在被建構中的能指。這個懸置的空白,恰恰是全篇文章真正的修辭動力來源:文本的每一個論證、每一個嘲諷、每一個自我矮化的姿態,都圍繞著這個**尚未到來因而無法被固定意義的中心空缺**旋轉。而這正是德希達意義下「延異」最赤裸的展演——意義的最終確立被無限期延後到一個結構性上永遠無法抵達的「之後」。

**結語**

解構閱讀最終顯示:大頭B這篇文章之所以耐讀,不在於它提出了什麼穩固的立場(無論是支持或反對馬克思主義),而在於它的每一個修辭策略——自我矮化、隱喻、二元對立的搭建——都在文本內部同時攜帶著使自己崩解的種子。這篇「僅傑拙作」與其說是一篇失敗地想要嚴肅論證的文章,不如說是一次(或許連作者自己都未必意識到的)對「嚴肅論證」這整套建制本身的解構操演。


## English

### A Deconstructive Reading

**I. The Text's Self-Deconstruction: "Unlearned and Untalented" as Strategic Modesty/Subversion**

A Derridean reading first asks whether a gap opens between a text's **rhetorical posture** and its **argumentative logic**. Big-Head B opens by positioning himself as "unlearned and untalented," "utterly lost" — yet this very disclaimer is precisely what a deconstructive reading should most suspect. Because immediately afterward, Big-Head B proceeds to offer a rather sharp, even incisive critique of "academic Marxism" (the abuse of dialectic, thesis-antithesis-synthesis reduced to template, the relativity of resource scarcity). Between the _claim_ of ignorance and the _analytical capacity_ the text actually displays, there opens a **supplementary gap** (_supplément_) — the modesty is not simple humility but a necessary "supplement" that allows a voice originally excluded from the institution of "the academic" to speak at the institution's margin without being immediately absorbed and neutralized by it. In other words, the persona "Big-Head B" is itself a deconstructive gesture: he must first _not be_ a scholar in order to speak to scholars at all.

**II. Loosening the Binary Oppositions: Who Is the Center, Who the Margin?**

On its surface, the text sustains a series of binary oppositions: academic/common-sense, theory/life, elite/Big-Head B, truth/nonsense. What a deconstructive reading must ask is: within each opposition, which term is secretly granted priority (the center), and which is demoted to the derivative, secondary, supplementary position (the margin)? On the surface, "academic Marxism" occupies the center (holding discursive authority, academic standing, the qualification for "theoretical criticism"), while Big-Head B situates himself at the margin ("not qualified," "nonsense"). But the entire rhetorical strategy of the text works precisely to **invert** this hierarchy of center and margin: it is exactly this voice that claims to be marginal, ignorant, humble, which ends up delivering the sharpest deconstruction of the center — exposing the hollow, "anything goes" universality of dialectic, exposing the paradoxical structure of "democratic dictatorship," exposing the violence concealed beneath the rhetoric of the "benevolent dictator." Center and margin thus keep exchanging places, never settling into stability — this is precisely what Derrida calls _différance_ at work: meaning is never present, but constantly deferred, displaced onto the next term in the opposition.

**III. Dialectic's Own Self-Deconstruction: An Operation the Text Has Already Performed**

It's worth noting that this essay has, in a sense, **already deconstructed its own object** prior to any external deconstructive reading. Beastmaster's drunken line — that dialectic, pushed onto "everything in the cosmos and society," ends up rationalizing any means whatsoever — is itself a proto-deconstruction of Hegelian-Marxist dialectic: dialectic claims to synthesize opposites (thesis/antithesis) into a higher unity (synthesis), but Big-Head B points out that this operation of "synthesis" can in fact be repeated indefinitely, extended without limit — each "synthesis" can always be re-designated as a new "thesis," generating a new "antithesis" in turn. This means dialectic can never truly arrive at a stable terminus (Absolute Spirit, the end of history in communism); it is only **an endless chain of supplements** (_chaîne de suppléments_) — the interchange of "principal" and "secondary" contradiction is precisely the most naked staging of this chain: the secondary contradiction supplements, replaces, and ultimately takes the place of the principal one, but this replacement never terminates, because the very opposition of "secondary" and "principal" is itself unstable, endlessly redistributable at the whim of power.

**IV. The Metaphor Runs Wild: The Casino as an Uncontrollable Slippage of Signifiers**

A deconstructive reading pays special attention to moments when a text's metaphor **escapes the author's control**. "Society is like a casino" seems, at first, meant to satirize totalitarian manipulation of "absolute equality" (stacking the deck in advance so everyone gets cards of the same value) — but once written down, this metaphor produces what Derrida calls a "slippage of the signifier" (_glissement du signifiant_): casino, house, gambler, gun, rules of the game — this entire chain of signifiers begins to operate on its own, even beginning to **deconstruct, in reverse, the very critical position the author originally meant to establish**. If the whole of society — including the position of "common sense" that Big-Head B himself occupies — is already part of the casino, then is Big-Head B himself not simply another participant "holding a poor hand" at the table, rather than a subject genuinely standing outside the casino to critique it? The essay's final line — "otherwise no one would bother running a casino" — paradoxically implies that even the most radical critic (the one who pulls a gun and sets himself up as the new house) ultimately only **reproduces the very same structure of power**, rather than truly escaping it. The text thus unwittingly deconstructs, at its own ending, the clean line it set up at the beginning between critic and the criticized.

**V. The Text's Undecidable Point of Suspension: 1997**

Finally, the temporal marker "1997 is near," read deconstructively, discloses itself as an **undecidable** (_un indécidable_) — it functions as the implicit terminus toward which the text's entire argument is oriented (every act of political participation, every salute to "tragic heroes," points toward this not-yet-arrived moment), yet it is never given any determinate content within the text itself: what, exactly, will happen after 1997? The text does not say, and cannot say, because at the moment of writing it is itself a signifier not yet decided, still under construction. This suspended blank is, in fact, the true rhetorical engine of the entire essay: every argument, every gibe, every gesture of self-deprecation revolves around this **central absence that has not yet arrived and therefore cannot be fixed in meaning**. This is _différance_ in its most naked performance — the final establishment of meaning indefinitely deferred toward a structurally unreachable "afterward."

**Conclusion**

What a deconstructive reading ultimately shows is that this essay by Big-Head B rewards attention not because it stakes out any stable position (for or against Marxism), but because every one of its rhetorical strategies — self-deprecation, metaphor, the construction of binary oppositions — simultaneously carries within it the seed of its own collapse. This "clumsy little effort" is less a failed attempt at serious argument than it is — perhaps without the author even realizing it — a deconstructive performance directed at the entire institution of "serious argument" itself.

#### 三、核心論點分析

**（一）對馬克思主義的批判——務實的懷疑主義**

大頭B對馬克思主義的批評，不是意識形態上的反共立場，而是一種務實的、以人性為基礎的懷疑主義。他的核心論點可以歸結為三點：

將這篇文章置於整個文本系列中，它扮演了一個關鍵的**解壓閥**的角色。

在《死神》的黑暗、《論基督徒》的憤怒、《太初有道》的末世、《反思/典範》的沉重哲學思辨之間，大頭B的聲音提醒我們：這個作者不只是一個在深淵邊緣孤獨搏鬥的存在主義者，他也是一個**活在香港1983年的具體歷史時刻中的人**——有朋友（BeastMaster，一起摸著酒杯底談馬克思的人），有政治關懷，有幽默感，有能力用笑聲觸碰痛苦。

大頭B的存在，使GODOG的圖像更為完整，也更為人性。

那個在沙漠中呼喊的人，也曾與朋友摸著酒杯底，談笑風生。

## English
## Critical Analysis and Commentary: "Big-Head B on Marxism"

#### I. Positioning the Text: A Completely New Voice

Within the entire body of work, this essay is a thorough anomaly.

In R. Incognito/GODOG's other writings, we hear a soul wrestling alone at the edge of a philosophical abyss — elevated language, intense emotion, dense imagery, saturated with existentialist weight. But the voice of "Big-Head B" is light, self-deprecating, streetwise, deliberately substituting ordinary vernacular for academic terminology, using Cantonese humor to deflate ideological seriousness.

Yet this lightness is a carefully engineered lightness.

Big-Head B is another mask worn by R. Incognito — or more precisely, his **mirror of self-critique**. By performing the role of an "uneducated" outsider, the author gains freedom to speak certain truths in ways that would feel too heavy in academic language.

---
#### II. Narrative Strategy

**The Function of the Big-Head B Persona**

Big-Head B presents himself as a natural science student with shallow knowledge of social theory, afflicted with a "phobia" of academic symbols. This setup is rhetorically intelligent and simultaneously accomplishes several things.

First, it establishes distance from academic hegemony — by declaring himself unqualified, he paradoxically gains freedom to criticize the qualified. This is classic **ironic humility**, deeply rooted in Chinese literary tradition, from Lu Xun's Ah Q to Wang Shuo's hooligan narrators.

Second, it creates a dialogic space with R. Incognito. Big-Head B explicitly names R. Incognito and BeastMaster, questioning whether they have "fallen into their own traps" — this is the author conducting internal self-criticism of his own thinking, a rare form of intellectual honesty.

Third, it broadens the essay's readership. Within the context of the undergraduate publication, Big-Head B's register is more inclusive than R. Incognito's, more genuinely inviting ordinary students into the conversation.

---

#### III. Analysis of Core Arguments

**1. On Marxism — Pragmatic Skepticism**

Big-Head B's critique of Marxism is not an ideological anti-communist position but a pragmatic, human-nature-based skepticism. His core argument rests on three pillars.

First, **the eternal gap between theory and practice**: all versions of Marxism produce impressive academic analysis but limited capacity for actual social change. The Russian and Chinese revolutions initially filled left-wing intellectuals with hope; reality ultimately shattered those dreams. This observation carries historical depth and represents a remarkably clear-eyed political judgment in the 1983 Hong Kong context.

Second, **the double-edged nature of dialectics**: the insight attributed to BeastMaster — that Marxism extends dialectics to all phenomena in the universe, with the result that any means can be rationalized through dialectical reasoning — is one of the most precise critiques of Marxist methodology available. Dialectics, as a cognitive tool, becomes a universal ideological rubber stamp once elevated into an all-explaining framework, capable of justifying any action including violence and oppression. This critique resonates academically with Karl Popper's criticism of Marxism's unfalsifiability.

Third, **the inescapability of the human nature problem**: the ideal of "from each according to his ability, to each according to his need" fails to adequately account for human laziness and greed. Big-Head B's subtlety here lies in refusing the simple "human nature is evil" position, instead arguing that even if human nature can be changed, the mechanism of change necessarily requires some form of coercion — and coercion's logical terminus is dictatorship. This chain of reasoning is among the most compact and powerful critiques of utopian politics available.

**2. The Casino Metaphor**

This is the essay's most original theoretical contribution.

"Government is the house dealer, everyone is playing poker" — this metaphor carries multiple resonances in 1983 Hong Kong.

It captures **structural inequality of opportunity**: some are born with good hands, some with bad, and the difference in cards far exceeds any difference in skill. This is an effective deconstruction of the liberal myth of equal opportunity.

It simultaneously critiques **egalitarian utopianism**: the dealer who pre-sorts the cards to give everyone equal point values destroys the excitement of the game and produces passive, dispirited players. This critiques coercive equality as purchasing sameness at the cost of freedom and vitality.

It preserves **anger at injustice**: the gambler who shoots the dealer and takes over the house is the metaphor for revolution — the author regards this impulse with a mixture of understanding and reservation.

The metaphor's limitation is that it reduces politics to a zero-sum game, eliding possibilities of cooperation and non-confrontational institutional reform. But as a thought experiment appearing in a 1983 undergraduate publication, its originality and insight are genuinely impressive.

**3. The Ghost of 1997**

The essay's repeated references to 1997 — "One Country Two Systems, please take note," "the first people to be struggled against after 1997 will be precisely these activists" — display a remarkably clear-eyed and prescient political consciousness for 1983.

The author describes democracy advocates as "tragic heroes," his tone mixing respect, sorrow, and a certain sense of helplessness. Viewed from forty years of subsequent history, this prediction proved to carry extraordinary foresight.

---

#### IV. Relationship to the R. Incognito Intellectual System

**The Function of Self-Critique**

Big-Head B's question — whether R. Incognito and BeastMaster are "performing their nihilism, perversion, and violence in the pages of the publication" — is the sharpest self-criticism anywhere in the entire body of work.

This points toward a genuine danger: **the self-indulgence of intellectuals**. Writing like _Death God_, however authentic as an expression of real suffering, raises the question: is it also performance? Is there, to some degree, an aestheticization of crisis, an identity-construction through pain?

Big-Head B does not answer this question. But its presence proves that R. Incognito maintained critical distance from his own intellectual tendencies — a rare form of intellectual honesty.

**The Complementarity of Two Voices**

R. Incognito provides depth; Big-Head B provides breadth. R. Incognito inhabits philosophical heights; Big-Head B stands on political ground. R. Incognito faces eternity; Big-Head B faces 1997. Together, the two voices constitute a complete portrait of a public intellectual.

---

#### V. Overall Significance Within the Text Series

Placed within the entire body of work, this essay functions as a crucial **pressure release valve**.

Between the darkness of _Death God_, the anger of _On Christians_, the apocalyptic scale of _In the Beginning Was the Word_, and the heavy philosophical reflection of _Reflection/Paradigm_, the voice of Big-Head B reminds us: this author is not only a solitary existentialist wrestling at the edge of the abyss. He is also **a person living in the concrete historical moment of Hong Kong in 1983** — with friends (BeastMaster, someone to share a drink with while discussing Marx), with political commitments, with a sense of humor, with the capacity to touch pain with laughter.

Big-Head B makes the picture of GODOG more complete, and more human.

The person who cried out unanswered in the desert also sat with a friend over drinks, talking and laughing about the state of the world.
