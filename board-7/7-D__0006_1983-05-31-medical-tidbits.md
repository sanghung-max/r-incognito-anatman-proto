### 學術時裝展 

#### 學術時裝

隨着社會政治潮流及個人存在處境的轉變,新款式的學術時裝將會流行。當大部份知識份子都穿上流行「新款」時裝後這些所謂新裝再不能顯出其獨特性及革命性,更「新款」的時裝相繼由學術時裝設計家推出,有些款式太革命性,不為社會時勢所容納,另一些却太保守,結果也被知識份子所淘汰。

作為「學術/符號/文化」的推銷員,我們會介紹各種不同的學術文化理論及研究結果,使消費者可以選擇自己喜歡的時裝。我們也歡迎「學術/符號/文化」的消費者度身訂做他們喜愛的衣服,保證是 tailor-made,用料上乘,使消費者稱心滿意。

七十年代末期的知識份子都以引用Structural Marxism,符號學、系統學等爲榮。Barthes, Althusser, Wilden, Foucault 等都成為「前衛」知識份子的的英雄偶像。他們靠著PROBLEMATIC, ISA, ARTICULATION, SIGNIFIER/SIGNIFIED, NEGATIVE DIALECTICS 之類的 JARGON 來表現自己對事物分析的獨特性及批判性,建立自我的形象。

可惜的是這些「前衛」知識份子的學術時裝款式不多(小得可憐),他們雖然很尖銳很 penetrating,但對社會批評只能停滯在經濟/政治架構及文化批判的層面,將人類的複雜性問題約化爲新馬克斯主義的理論。而他們所論述的問題都沒有提及其他學派的說法,就算是在新馬克斯理論的研究也頗狹窄,直至現在,很小知識份子討論 Lacan, Apel 或 Lorenzer,更小學者去發掘非新左的理論如 hermeneutics, phenomenology, cybernetics  或 information Theory等。 
另一方面,以往「前衛」的知識份子大都忽略了自然科學及應用科學技術對人類的影響,但還要一知半解地討論自然科學的方法論及哲學。學術時裝展希望透過一兩版來介紹各門科學的新發展,我們更加希望這個時裝展能夠培養大家對各門各派理論的興趣, 增加自然科學及社會科學/人文科學的對話。

### AIDS
近來許多針對「修改同性戀法例」的討論都說及種神秘的病症 AIDS (Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome) 這種病使患者的免疫能力消失,身體某種白血球(叫做 Lymphocytes)的免疫反應受影響,導致癌病及其它病症。這種病是在1979年發現,71%患者是男同性戀者,其他患者是吸毒者、和患病男性有性接觸的女人及海地人等。
直至83年5月,美國已有1450人患上此症其中558人死亡。很多醫學工作者都懷疑這種疾病是角一超過濾性病毒(叫做 retroviruses 引起,這類病毒可能抑制身體免疫系統正常的運作 。他們相信這種病是由血液、精液、唾液或黏液傳染见者致死的原因大多是 Kaposi's sarcoma (75%患者兩年內死亡),消化系統癌症及肺炎等併發症。醫生利用干擾素 (interferon) 輔助病人的免疫系統,抵抗 AIDS 帶來的癌症。
(資料輯自'DISCOVER" MAY, JULY 1983)

### HERPES
談起疱疹,很多人都會感到害怕,加上新聞界的 「加鹽加醋」式的宣傳,說在海灘或厕所板都可以傳染到此病………………好吧!我會使你們有Herpes Phobia 靜靜地告訴你,根據一些用血清實驗的醫學報告, 城市中百份之九十五的成年人都會因疱疹病毒而感染到這種傳染病!
疱疹病毒(Herpes simplex virus) 和引起水痘 (Chicken pox)的病毒 (Varicella-zoster virus)同屬一類。疱疹病毒可以分為兩類,就是一型(HSV1)及二型 V2)。一型所引起的病徵集中在口腔面部及神經線, 而二型是影響生殖器官及初生嬰兒。
疱疹二型所引起的疾病 (herpes geni-talis) 可能是最常見的性病,佔通過性交傳染的疾病 (sexually transmitted di-sease) 13%。
在海灘傳染到的疱疹是疱疹一型,不是「性病」 !不過患上疱疹對生命也會有危險,因為它可能引起其他併發症如腦炎及腦膜炎。大家還要小心。
(資料取自 Notes on Infectious Diseases, Ed. A.P. Ball, 1982)

### Academic Fashion Show

### Academic Fashion

As social and political currents, and the circumstances of individual existence, change, new styles of academic fashion come into vogue. Once most intellectuals have put on the fashionable “new” attire, however, such supposedly new clothes can no longer display their distinctiveness or revolutionary character. Still “newer” fashions are then launched by the designers of academic fashion. Some styles are too revolutionary to be accommodated by the prevailing social climate; others are too conservative and are consequently discarded by intellectuals as well.

As salespeople of “academia/signs/culture,” we shall introduce a variety of academic and cultural theories and research findings, so that consumers may choose the fashions they prefer. We also welcome consumers of “academia/signs/culture” to have their favourite garments made to measure. We guarantee that they will be tailor-made and made from the finest materials, so that consumers will be entirely satisfied.

By the end of the 1970s, intellectuals took pride in citing Structural Marxism, semiotics, systems theory, and the like. Barthes, Althusser, Wilden, and Foucault became the heroic idols of “avant-garde” intellectuals. By deploying jargon such as **PROBLEMATIC**, **ISA**, **ARTICULATION**, **SIGNIFIER/SIGNIFIED**, and **NEGATIVE DIALECTICS**, they displayed the distinctiveness and critical edge of their analyses and constructed their own intellectual images.

Unfortunately, the range of academic fashions available to these “avant-garde” intellectuals was very limited—pitifully so. Although they were sharp and penetrating, their social criticism could only remain at the level of economic/political structures and cultural critique, reducing the problems of human complexity to neo-Marxist theory. The issues they discussed made no reference to the arguments of other schools of thought. Even their engagement with neo-Marxist theory itself was rather narrow.

To this day, few intellectuals discuss Lacan, Apel, or Lorenzer; even fewer scholars explore theories outside the neo-Left, such as hermeneutics, phenomenology, cybernetics, or information theory.

On the other hand, earlier “avant-garde” intellectuals generally neglected the impact of natural science and applied scientific technology on human life, while still discussing—only half-understood—the methodology and philosophy of natural science. _Academic Fashion Show_ hopes, through one or two pages at a time, to introduce new developments in different sciences. More importantly, we hope that this fashion show will cultivate readers’ interest in theories from different disciplines and schools, and increase dialogue between natural science and the social sciences/humanities.

### AIDS

Recently, many discussions concerning the “amendment of homosexual law” have mentioned a mysterious illness called AIDS—Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome. This disease causes sufferers to lose their immune capacity. The immune response of a type of white blood cell, called lymphocytes, is affected, resulting in cancer and other illnesses.

The disease was discovered in 1979. Seventy-one percent of patients were gay men; other patients included drug users, women who had sexual contact with infected men, and Haitians.

By May 1983, 1,450 people in the United States had contracted the illness, 558 of whom had died. Many medical workers suspected that the disease was caused by a retrovirus, a class of virus that might suppress the normal functioning of the body’s immune system. They believed that the disease could be transmitted through blood, semen, saliva, or mucus.

Most deaths were caused by Kaposi’s sarcoma—of which 75 percent of patients died within two years—cancers of the digestive system, pneumonia, and other complications. Doctors used interferon to support patients’ immune systems in resisting AIDS-related cancers.

_Information compiled from_ Discover, _May and July 1983._

### Herpes

When herpes is mentioned, many people become frightened. Added to this is the media’s tendency to “season” the story with exaggeration, claiming that one can contract the disease at a beach or from a toilet seat. Well then—I shall give you herpes phobia. Quietly, let me tell you that, according to certain medical reports using serological tests, 95 percent of adults in cities will be infected with this contagious disease because of the herpes virus!

The herpes simplex virus and the varicella-zoster virus, which causes chickenpox, belong to the same family. Herpes viruses may be divided into two types: Type I, HSV-1, and Type II, HSV-2. Type I causes symptoms concentrated around the mouth, face, and nerves, while Type II affects the genitals and newborn infants.

The disease caused by Herpes Type II—herpes genitalis—may be the most common sexually transmitted disease, accounting for 13 percent of diseases transmitted through sexual intercourse.

Herpes contracted at the beach is Herpes Type I, not a “venereal disease”! Nevertheless, contracting herpes can be dangerous to life, because it may cause complications such as encephalitis and meningitis. Everyone should still take care.

_Information taken from_ Notes on Infectious Diseases, _ed. A. P. Ball, 1982._

### AIDS and Herpes Phobia

The American media have made a great deal of these two diseases, provoking a nationwide phobia. _The New York Times_ even ran the headline:

> MERE CONTACT MAY SPREAD AIDS.

Hong Kong newspapers have likewise made herpes into a major news story. Some commentators opposed to amending homosexual law have also used AIDS to reinforce their arguments. Their chief purpose is to generate sensational forms of mass phobia in order to achieve their own ends—perhaps to increase readership or to promote a particular moral ideology.

From these observations, we can see not only whether the supposedly “objective and fair-minded” news media really are so; we must also ask why phobias arise at all.

### Missionary Position and Women’s Liberation

What is the **missionary position**? It is the traditional sexual position: man on top, woman beneath. Is man the conqueror? Is woman the conquered? Is man the exploiter? What is the women’s liberation movement? Is it a reversal of positions—woman on top and man underneath during sex? Or is it the struggle for more political and economic rights already defined by a male society?

The women’s liberation movement often falls into the trap of formalism. What feminists pursue are merely superficial interests, while neglecting their own feelings and psychology. Even if women can obtain equal opportunity, equal treatment, and other equal material conditions, they cannot be said to be liberated if their mentality or ideology remains unchanged.

A change—or revolution—in women’s feelings and attitudes toward themselves and toward men is a precondition for women’s liberation. This psychological transformation does not mean that feminists should become “big women” or “strong women”—note the biased connotations of the phrase “strong woman.” Yet many women who embrace women’s liberation overreact, becoming emotionally hostile to men and unable to regard them as equal counterparts.

Rationally, they may demand formal equality, but deep down they still fear men and seek to protect themselves. The result is a need to prove that men are “inferior” or “worthless”—the text leaves the exact term unstated, perhaps deliberately—just as some macho men do in reverse. Unconsciously, they view all relations between men and women as positional struggles for power, directly or indirectly producing tragedy.

Conversely, the problems of romantic love between men and women are permeated by too many political games of power. So-called courtship consists in controlling one’s own emotions and those of the other person—consciously or unconsciously—and in shaping and manipulating the other’s speech and action so that they conform to one’s own wishes. Is this mutual accommodation?

> Women’s liberation entails a critical and radical re-evaluation of the manifold relations between the sexes.

Most important is the content and nature of relations between men and women, rather than their form.

Put simply, if women’s liberation is to succeed, it must simultaneously struggle for both objective and subjective conditions. Its strategy should proceed step by step and should not be excessively radical.

Feminists must be careful about a separation between reason and feeling—for example, “loving” a man who is completely unsuitable for oneself—which makes one’s life painful. Therefore, the essential component of women’s liberation is the discovery and articulation of women’s emotional structure—its origin, history, and development—as well as reflection upon their immediate feelings arising from interpersonal processes.
