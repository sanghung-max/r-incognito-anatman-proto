港大的「主流意識」及風氣很容易將個人的特點磨平,使人處於一個「靈性空虛」的境況,部份大學生也是透過 參予政治或宗教活動來填補心靈的真空 ,我不是反對這些活動,但有很多大學 生卻完全沒有反省他們的情況,只是下 意識地參予,不經思索就接受了被給予 的「意義」及「解釋」。

我並不是要別人追隨我走,也不代 表「絕對真理」,我只要求大家對自己 選擇的道路負責,不要將責任推之於「 環境」、「現實」、「形象」或其它外 在因素上。大學是一處訓練個人性格及 思想的地方,我們若要追求「理想」、 「民主」、「自由」………………,就要做好第 一步,對自己來一個全面的探索,而不 是渾渾 圍地讀書或攪社運學運。

**Academic Section — Unlearned and Incompetent: Is This a Resignation Piece?**

I do not know what I may appear to the world, but to myself I seem to have been only a boy playing on the seashore, and diverting myself in now and then finding a smoother pebble or a prettier shell than ordinary, whilst the great ocean of truth lay all undiscovered before me.

—Isaac Newton

Doing scholarship is a way of living: approaching everyday things with a spirit of doubt and uncertainty, demanding of oneself a profound way of living. I do not like half-hearted play-acting that casually introduces academic theories and ideas while completely ignoring their essence. Still less do I admire the absence of independent thinking and analysis—lingering only on the surface of a problem or theory, unable to turn inward and examine one’s own methods of cognition and processes of thought.

If I were to evaluate this year’s academic section’s “achievements,” I can only say: unlearned and incompetent. As an editor, I must take responsibility—I failed to fulfill my own promises…

Is this a resignation piece? I still have not finished. This feeling is like being suspended in mid-air—“asking heaven yields nothing, in the end I cannot.” A little bit obsessive. I will keep my promise firmly in mind, yet I still cannot complete it. My mirror is shattered.

I seem to have left the donkey’s den, watching from a height how I myself go in and out of the 《Undergrad》 room. Before every issue of 《Undergrad》 is published—all I can feel is my impending and imminent death…… I don’t want to burden anybody with my personal feelings. I simply don’t want to talk.

I do not like people who, once fallen, cannot get up by themselves. In 《Undergrad》 I hope everyone is equal, that every person can take care of themselves, needing neither to be feast-fed nor bottle-fed.

I am very harsh on myself, but…… perhaps I should retire. I am tired. People in 《Undergrad》 say I have aged thirty years; looking back, there is more white in my hair.

I really do not know how to write any further…… Just shattered.

**Freedom of Speech · 《Undergrad》 · Students’ Union**

Certain Students’ Union officers have pointed out that 《Undergrad》 is not “open” enough, that it only knows how to destroy and offers no “constructive” proposals. I think they confuse the concept of freedom of speech. Although I oppose their understanding of certain things, I will not deprive them of the right to speak; I believe the 《Undergrad》 editorial board would not do so either. Being criticized naturally feels unpleasant, yet one must never, because the other side holds opposing views, regard them as “unrealistic” or intolerant of dissent.

One ideal of university education is to require students to cast doubt upon and analyze certain deep-rooted ideas, habits, or sentiments. To look at oneself and society with a critical and skeptical spirit is a painful matter, yet I believe it is an important process of self-growth. Much of what is called “constructive” is merely empty talk of blind conformity or clinging to convention; “constructiveness” is used to paper over every contradiction and to evade responsibilities and decisions that must be reached through deep thought and inner struggle. Many university students, because they cling to certain “experience,” “knowledge,” or “constructive” “ideals,” become self-satisfied, imagining they have found all truth, and even assume the posture of “authority” to act as moral arbiters over others.

Of course some people hope the Students’ Union can serve political ends, yet if that is elevated to the ultimate and highest goal, it is precisely to abandon the fundamental spirit of the university. I must reiterate: the university is a place of academic freedom; we should not sacrifice these principles in order to serve political, social, or economic interests.

Freedom of speech and an open medium are the preconditions of academic freedom, and also the necessary conditions of liberty and democracy. Therefore I absolutely do not approve of the 《Undergrad》 editors, on the basis of their own subjective values, politics, religion, or moral judgments, prohibiting the publication of controversial articles. Still less do I approve of the Students’ Union or university authorities using pretexts such as “damaging or distorting the image,” “corrupting morals,” etc., to suppress speech and publication (I still remember how the Students’ Union used administrative power to ban the sale of a certain issue of 《Baixing》; the “cooperative” incident). Only thus can power be dispersed and not concentrated in the hands of a small number of decision-makers or administrators.

Some former 《Undergrad》 and Students’ Union high cadres think this year’s 《Undergrad》 has neglected its “responsibility” toward fellow students. Others believe certain articles will create more “identity crises,” causing this generation of university students to lose objects of identification and leadership (for example the mainstream consciousness of social movements, student movements, and the Students’ Union). But I must ask them in return: before “taking action,” did they ever think of their own responsibility? In reality every decision or word and deed of each of us influences and changes both ourselves and others—have we thought carefully about that? Why can we not endure an “identity crisis”? Why can we not endure “disunity of thought”? If we wish to change ourselves and to promote and realize “democratization,” an “identity crisis” is an important process.

The “mainstream consciousness” and atmosphere of HKU easily grind down individual characteristics, leaving people in a state of “spiritual emptiness.” Some university students fill this vacuum of the soul by participating in political or religious activities. I do not oppose these activities, yet many students never reflect on their own situation; they participate only subconsciously, accepting the “meanings” and “explanations” handed to them without thinking.

I do not ask others to follow my path, nor do I represent “absolute truth.” I only require that everyone take responsibility for the road they themselves have chosen, and not shift responsibility onto “environment,” “reality,” “image,” or other external factors. The university is a place for training individual character and thought. If we wish to pursue “ideals,” “democracy,” “freedom”…… we must take the first step properly: undertake a thorough exploration of ourselves, rather than reading or engaging in social and student movements in a muddled, unthinking way.

Is this a resignation piece

Hung Yat-sang
