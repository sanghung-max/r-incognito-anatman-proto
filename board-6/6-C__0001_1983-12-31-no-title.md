## 觀景 / vista




### GPT-5.6 Luna | 2026-09-17 | Critical Commentary

This poem is interesting precisely because it does **not** behave like a conventional character portrait. It appears to use “she” as a constructed figure through whom the writer investigates a problem of **selfhood, agency, self-deception, and existential absence**.

### Critical commentary

The poem's central paradox is established very early: she appears extraordinarily resistant and self-controlled—“Nothing can move her”—yet is simultaneously “like a fragile reed.” Her apparent indifference and austerity therefore conceal, rather than eliminate, extreme emotional vulnerability. The poem keeps returning to this contradiction.

The most important passage, in my reading, is:

> “she makes them out of her own choice  
> and bears upon them her own.”

This gives the poem its existential architecture. Her circumstances are not simply imposed upon her. She **chooses**, constructs, and then inhabits them. But the very choices through which she creates herself become constraints. She subsequently rationalizes those circumstances until they appear to confirm the nature she has already constructed for herself.

That is why the poem repeatedly invokes **illusion, justification, rationalization, seriousness, choice, and self-deception**. The problem is not simply that she believes something false. More radically, she has developed a system in which almost anything can be incorporated into the story she tells herself. “Say they are true—and what's the difference?” is almost an epistemological collapse: if subjective conviction can substitute for correspondence with reality, then the distinction between truth and self-created illusion becomes unstable.

### The deeper subject: an absent self

The poem eventually moves beyond ordinary psychological conflict:

> “All her life an illusion  
> Of who she is, of who she is, but where is she?”

That repetition—**“who she is … where is she?”**—is probably the poem's deepest question.

The first question assumes that there is a person whose identity can be discovered. The second undermines that assumption. Perhaps there is no stable “she” underneath the accumulated choices, defenses, dreams, pride, rationalizations, and performances.

This makes the final:

> “As though she is absent.”

much more significant than a statement of loneliness.

She is not merely **alone**. She is potentially **absent from herself**.

### Why the dream imagery matters

The sequence

> “She’s but a dream within,  
> but cannot stand others dreaming”
> 
> “there are just dreams of her dream”

creates another recursive structure. She does not merely live in an illusion; she becomes conscious of herself through layers of representation—her dream, her dream of herself, and eventually her doubt about the dreams of her dream.

The possibility of awakening therefore becomes frightening rather than reassuring:

> “Someday she may wake up  
> with all her past a dream.”

If awakening reveals that the past itself was constructed, then there is no guaranteed authentic self waiting behind the illusion. **Reality may simply expose another layer of uncertainty.**

### Intent

I would distinguish **probable literary intent** from something we can establish biographically.

The probable intent is **not primarily to condemn “her.”** The unusually persistent attention to her internal contradictions suggests an attempt to _enter and map a consciousness that cannot reconcile autonomy with vulnerability, desire with restraint, and self-knowledge with self-deception._

There is also a strong possibility that “she” functions as a **persona or displaced self** rather than simply as a woman being described. The grammatical choice of third person is important. Instead of saying _I_, the poem can examine the subject from a distance: _she_ can be simultaneously the observed object and the observing consciousness.

That creates an intriguing double movement:

**I observe her → I understand her → I accuse her → I discover that I may be her.**

But the text alone cannot establish whether this was consciously intended as autobiographical displacement. That should remain an archival hypothesis rather than a conclusion.

### What makes the poem compelling—and imperfect

Its greatest strength is also its greatest formal weakness.

The poem has a powerful **conceptual coherence** but relatively loose linguistic control. Phrases such as “la lelle indifference,” “induration (?),” “contriving without knowledge,” and the repeated syntactic disruptions create uncertainty about whether particular effects are deliberate poetic fragmentation, English-language experimentation, or simply an unfinished early draft.

I would **not automatically normalize those irregularities**. In an archival reading, they may be evidence of the writer thinking through the poem in real time. The poem's language sometimes seems to enact the very instability it describes: syntax loses its footing while the subject loses hers.

There is also considerable repetition of the same conceptual vocabulary—illusion, choice, justification, dream, self, reality. A later, more technically mature poet might compress this substantially. But compression would also risk destroying something important: the poem's feeling of **circular entrapment**.

The reader is made to experience the same problem as the subject: every apparent explanation generates another explanation.

### Overall interpretation

I would read the poem as an **early existential phenomenology of a divided self**.

Its fundamental question is not:

**“Why does this woman behave badly?”**

nor even:

**“Why is she unhappy?”**

It is closer to:

**“If the self continually constructs the circumstances, explanations, dreams, and identities through which it understands itself, how can it ever distinguish the authentic self from the fiction it has created?”**

And the poem does not resolve that question.

Indeed, its final movement suggests that the terrifying possibility is not discovering that _she has been living a lie_, but discovering that **there may be no completely authentic “she” behind the lies**.

That makes the final “absence” the culmination of the entire poem rather than merely its mood: **the subject spends the poem trying to define herself, while the poem progressively removes the stable ground from which that definition could be made.**

### R. Incognito | circa Dec 1983
### Poem - No Title 

A-D__0004 p.28-30

circa 1983-1984

  
Nothing can move her in her way, 

not even the bitter-sweet passion, 

or the ot gaudy day.

A woman born of the loftiest inclination (induration?) 

of indifference and style 

But she is like a fragile reed. 

So proves to herself 

Extremities, 

to take and make the risks life is. 

Admit only the simple or the less,

or the more. 

Or just pretend to accept, 

those hard facts of her life 

Constantly eluding herself, 

and save all delusions in her chest. 

Say they are true - and what's the difference? 

To live without living, 

To love without being in love 

and weaves her pride in a cobweb. 

Half-hysterical, half- possessed 

contriving without knowledge,, 

or without any restraint at all. 

She plays dice with her life,

and escapes from herself 

seems to her an indulgent phase 

A deep and sensitive soul within 

yielding to no counterpart, 

all at a loss. 

Without reason or hope, 

in a timeless mind. 

Whence hatred and guilt turned to simpler passion

diffuse and foreign as ghost. 

But she’s the real culprit of her soul 

tormenting herself with that la lelle indifference, 

with that great tragic sense 

still thinks that she's not deceiving and deceived in any way

choosing a way to her contrary

in a naughtier and haughtier style, 

still maintaining her austerity, 

Denying but not forgetting, 

for all she has done and felt. 

And feels that she is serious  herself 

making all kinds of justifications 

or rationalizations as such 

have herself believe in those 

So entrenched within her contrived circumstances

deeply convinced of her own nature. 

she makes them out of her own choice

 and bears upon them her own. 

A perplexing creature as she is, 

acting out of instant feelings 

All her life an illusion

Of who she is, of who she is, but where is she?

She’s no timid ; nor simple 

so harsh in her determination 

not even promise herself. 

She’s but a dream within, 

but cannot stand others dreaming 

or lovig as innocent men do 

for there are just dreams of her dream

Someday she may wake up 

with all her past a dream. 

with that familiar emptiness and solitude

a feeling of not living before or after

And doubts of the dreams of her dream

How can she ever face

Her own reality,  and move beyond of who she has been

Or live in this knitted world.

As though she is absent.

**

- **#### 缺席的痕跡：** 最後的 “As though she is absent” 並非單純結論，而是整首詩一直存在的 structural trace：**主體越努力回答「who she is」，越暴露出沒有一個固定的「she」可供回答。**
    

## English

### GPT-5.6 Luna | 2026-09-17 14:26 PDT | Deconstruct

- **#### Binary instability:** The poem operates through interdependent oppositions—**indifference/passion, living/not living, truth/illusion, choice/constraint, dream/reality, self/absence**—yet each pole repeatedly leaks into its opposite.
    
- **#### The fracture of “choice”:** “She makes them out of her own choice” appears to establish agency, yet choice simultaneously produces “contrived circumstances” and self-deception. Agency therefore becomes both freedom-signifier and mechanism of confinement.
    
- **#### Semantic slippage:** Terms such as “serious,” “indifference,” “austerity,” and “rationalizations” appear to name stable traits while also functioning as retrospective narrative labels; no final semantic center can definitively stabilize “her.”
    
- **#### Trace of absence:** “As though she is absent” is not merely the ending but a **structural trace running through the poem**: the harder the text tries to answer “who she is,” the more it exposes the absence of a fixed “she” capable of being answered.
